# INTERREGNUM taste bible

Every concept must pass this filter before any script is written. It was written after the showrunner's note on
films 1–2 (2026-09-30): the loved works "are all not cliche when they came out… it's not that we can't use repeated
elements, it's just the core can't be something so common that people are tired and sick of seeing." Both films also
held one tempo from start to finish.

**Why a document, not a reminder.** A writer who learned from everything already written reaches first for the most
expected answer, and so do I. Trend summaries and "what people are talking about" are the median of the median. This
bible keeps that median out of the core of a film. It is fine as texture.

**How to use it**
- §1 and §2 show what freshness looked like in the works the showrunner loves: a new core, a familiar scaffold, niche
  true material, and time used as form. Read them for the *kind* of move, never to copy a surface.
- §3 is the ban list for our themes. A banned take may appear as texture, never as the core.
- §4 is how to find material that isn't already in everyone's feed.
- §5 is the test. Concept sessions read §2, §3 and §5; the judge scores every concept with §5 and rejects any below the
  bar.

Our films are fiction (see `bible/brief.md`). This document names real works and cites critics only to learn from them.

---

## 1. What made each loved work fresh when it came out

For each work: the core invention that was new at the time (*New*), the familiar scaffold it reused (*Reused*), the
specific human material it drew on (*Material*) and how it handles pacing and time (*Time*), with what creators and
critics said at release. Three patterns recur and feed §2. The new thing is a rule or a form, never a topic. The
scaffold is old on purpose. And many were attacked at release for the very choice that made them last.

### Nolan, Villeneuve and other time machines

**Interstellar (2014).** *New:* relativity as a parent–child melodrama. An hour on Miller's planet costs seven years at
home, and Cooper then watches 23 years of his children's messages in one sitting. The black hole was computed, not
designed: Kip Thorne's equations, rendered by DNEG, became a physics paper ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.03808)).
*Reused:* *2001*, the farmer-pilot Western, a haunted bookshelf. *Material:* real Dust Bowl survivors from Ken Burns's
documentary open the film, so testimony about a past catastrophe plays as oral history of a future one
([Collider](https://collider.com/christopher-nolan-interstellar-ken-burns-dust-bowl-documentary/)). *Time:* the launch
countdown starts while Cooper drives off the farm, so goodbye and liftoff are one gesture. TIME: physics "through a
daddy-daughter love story" ([TIME](https://time.com/3545770/interstellar-movie-review/)).

**Tenet (2020).** *New:* inversion as an action grammar. Forward and backward bodies collide inside one fight, mostly in
camera: stunt performers "were basically learning how to perform convincingly in reverse" ([befores &
afters](https://beforesandafters.com/2020/10/23/heres-how-the-effects-teams-on-tenet-pulled-off-those-crazy-time-bending-scenes/)).
The Sator square, a real Latin palindrome, is the architecture: after the midpoint the hero relives the first half from
the other side. *Reused:* Bond and Nolan's own heist machinery; Nolan worked "from a memory and a feeling of that genre,
rather than the specifics" ([Total Film](https://www.gamesradar.com/tenet-christopher-nolan-set-behind-the-scene/)).
*Material:* art freeports; a real 747 bought from a desert boneyard. *Time:* relentless from the opening siege, with
exposition at a sprint; in the second half the audience's memory of the first is the special effect. The common
complaint was dialogue buried in the mix
([Entertainment.ie](https://entertainment.ie/cinema/movie-news/tenet-sound-mixing-460957/)).

**The Odyssey (2026).** *New:* the first feature shot entirely on IMAX film, dialogue included, on a new quiet camera
([CineD](https://www.cined.com/how-christopher-nolan-shot-the-odyssey-entirely-on-imax-film-inside-the-new-keighley-camera/));
and myth as "a time of apparent magic", in which the gods act only through natural phenomena and Odysseus beats Circe by
his own wits. *Reused:* Homer's nested tellings; the sword-and-sandals epic it defines itself against. *Material:* the
cast learned to row and sail a replica longship, "shooting it kind of like a documentary"
([GamesRadar+](https://www.gamesradar.com/entertainment/action-movies/we-were-shooting-it-like-a-documentary-the-odyssey-cast-learned-to-sail-for-real-as-christopher-nolan-admits-it-was-tough-on-his-actors/));
the Bronze Age collapse, through a Sea Peoples theory the classicist Emily Wilson calls "now debunked"
([LRB](https://www.lrb.co.uk/the-paper/v48/n14/emily-wilson/an-uncomplicated-man)). *Time:* twenty years told as "a
series of elliptical short stories" by unreliable narrators ([Total
Film](https://www.gamesradar.com/entertainment/fantasy-movies/the-odyssey-review/)); the film stock itself set the tempo
(three-minute magazines, a three-hour cap).

**Arrival (2016).** *New:* grammar as the climax. Earlier contact films decoded a signal; here the decoding changes the
decoder, because the aliens' circular writing rewires Louise's sense of time. The opening "flashback" of a daughter's
life and death turns out to be a flash-forward, so the audience undergoes the shift itself. *Reused:* the first-contact
procedural (shells, an army camp, a countdown to war) and a Sapir–Whorf hypothesis linguists reject.
*Material:* field linguistics (consultant Jessica Coon: Louise works "as any good fieldworker would",
[Science & Film](https://scienceandfilm.org/articles/2802/alien-speak-linguist-dr-jessica-coon-on-villeneuves-arrival));
real Wolfram Language analysis on the monitors. *Time:* patient sessions inside a speeding
geopolitical clock; one Max Richter piece opens and closes the film, so the end loops to the start. TIME: "a movie
about aliens for people who don't like movies about aliens" ([TIME](https://time.com/4565975/amy-adams-arrival/)).

**Dune (2021) and Dune: Part Two (2024).** *New:* a monumental, analogue world in which sound is a lead sense: full-size
ornithopters, desert shot against sand-coloured screens, sand recorded with hydrophones, a score of bagpipes and
invented instruments. Part Two lights one planet in infrared black-and-white
([ASC](https://theasc.com/article/expanding-view-dune-part-two/)) and turns the chosen one into a warning: Chani walks
away. *Reused:* Herbert's sources (T. E. Lawrence, feudal houses), *Lawrence of Arabia*. *Material:* the novel grew from
Herbert's reporting on the USDA's effort to stop Oregon's dunes "moving across the highways" ([January
Magazine](http://www.januarymagazine.com/profiles/duneprofile.html)). *Time:* slow, silent approaches to enormous
objects; one story cut in half. Villeneuve: "When you adapt, you kill"
([TIME](https://time.com/6589871/denis-villeneuve-dune-part-two-interview/)).

**Blade Runner 2049 (2017).** *New:* the chosen one revoked. K and the audience are allowed to believe he is the
miracle child, then learn he isn't, and his last act is chosen as a nobody. Joi is love as a product feature, "a
program designed to fulfill desire", laid over another woman's body in the love scene
([Den of Geek](https://www.denofgeek.com/movies/denis-villeneuve-interview-blade-runner-2049-marketing-and-design/)).
*Reused:* the 1982 world, noir, Pinocchio. *Material:* the baseline test is an actors' Shakespeare-learning drill
turned into interrogation, on lines from Nabokov's *Pale Fire*. *Time:* slowness as presence ("Otherwise it's just
television!", Villeneuve); the same test heard twice measures K's drift. The complaint was length, "a long, largely
humourless experience" ([Total Film](https://www.gamesradar.com/blade-runner-2049-review/)), and it fell short at the
box office.

**Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind (2004).** *New:* backward chronology with a motor and a mutineer. Lacuna erases
Joel's memories newest-first, so the film runs from the break-up to the first meeting, and midway Joel rebels, hiding
Clementine in memories where she doesn't belong. The opening meet-cute is really their second first meeting. Memory
decay is done in camera: oversized sets so Carrey can play himself as a boy, one "memory light" on the camera. *Reused:*
boy-meets-girl. *Material:* Pierre Bismuth's unmade art piece, cards telling people they had been erased from someone's
memory
([Dazed](https://web.archive.org/web/20180704124204/http://www.dazeddigital.com/artsandculture/article/19247/1/a-secret-history-of-michel-gondry)).
*Time:* two clocks cross-cut, the technicians' night running forward while the memories run back. The studio worried,
but "it was important to us to keep that achronology" (Kaufman, [A.V.
Club](https://web.archive.org/web/20180628124758/https://www.avclub.com/michel-gondry-charlie-kaufman-1798208337)).

### Makoto Shinkai

**Your Name (2016).** *New:* a body-swap comedy that hides a three-year gap. For an hour the swaps are gags; then Taki
learns a comet destroyed Mitsuha's town three years earlier, and the gimmick becomes a rescue. Forgetting is the clock:
each swap erases names. *Reused:* a Heian tale of swapped siblings, named in the working title ([official
site](https://www.kiminona.com/production/)), and Shinkai's own lovers-apart template. *Material:* the 2011 Tōhoku
disaster recast as a comet ("there was something similar 1,000 years ago, which we all forgot about",
[Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/your-name-is-the-new-anime-by-the-next-hayao-miyazaki-japanese-director-makoto-shinkai/));
shrine braiding and mouth-chewed sake as time machinery; real Hida streets. *Time:* weeks compressed into a music video;
at the midpoint the swaps stop dead, and the second half is one night on two timelines. Shinkai aimed for what adults
"would call overdone, with too much music" ([Pia](https://kansai.pia.co.jp/interview/cinema/2016-09/kiminona.html),
translated).

**Weathering with You (2019).** *New:* refusing the sacrifice. The weather maiden must vanish to stop the rain; the
boy pulls her back anyway, and Tokyo stays under water. *Your Name* undid its disaster; this one keeps it. The miracle
is run as a teenage side hustle with a homemade booking site. *Reused:* the *Your Name* package, Murakami. *Material:*
Tokyo's topography (low districts that were sea before Edo); a cloud researcher as weather supervisor; real brands,
down to the occult monthly *Mu*. *Time:* rain is the meter, so every sunbreak is an event; snow falls in midsummer the
night she vanishes. Shinkai: "Should I make a film my critics will like… or should I make one they'll hate even
more?" ([Japan Times](https://features.japantimes.co.jp/makoto-shinkai-interview/)). Kotaku found the late choices
"downright selfish" ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/weathering-with-you-is-a-good-but-flawed-follow-up-to-1836520213)).

### Chinese imagination

**The Three-Body Problem (2006–08; English 2014).** *New:* first contact caused by a national trauma. Ye Wenjie watched
Red Guards beat her father to death; years later, at a secret radar base, she answers an alien warning and invites them
in. Earlier Chinese SF had aliens who "appeared as friends or mentors" (Liu,
[Reactor](https://reactormag.com/the-worst-of-all-possible-universes-and-the-best-of-all-possible-earths-three-body-and-chinese-science-fiction/)).
A VR game teaches alien physics through King Wen, Mozi and Newton, and 30 million soldiers become a human computer.
*Reused:* Clarke-style hard SF, SETI, a scientist-and-cop buddy plot. *Material:* Liu's childhood memory of gunshots at
night; his years as a power-plant software engineer. *Time:* a countdown appears in the hero's photographs, then in his
eyes. Ken Liu moved the 1967 chapters to the front; the author: "That is how I wanted it originally!"
([NYT](https://web.archive.org/web/20210101204028/https://www.nytimes.com/2019/12/03/magazine/ken-liu-three-body-problem-chinese-science-fiction.html)).

**The Wandering Earth (2019).** *New:* take the planet along. Against the Hollywood exodus, where "the hero always
ventures out into space to find a new home", ten thousand engines push Earth itself: "This comes from Chinese cultural
values — homeland, history and continuity" (Frant Gwo,
[THR](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/wandering-earth-director-making-chinas-first-sci-fi-blockbuster-1187681)).
A collective rescue replaces the lone hero. *Reused:* *Armageddon*, *Gravity*, a HAL-like AI, a father–son melodrama.
*Material:* an invented hundred-year history "sketching out developments for every year"; heavy-haul trucks; Spring
Festival, mahjong and the road slogan "safety first" carried into the future. *Time:* a relay of deadlines against
Jupiter's pull, released on Lunar New Year's Day. Slate: "Rarely does a film possess such a technocratic heart"; the
NYT: "manipulative sentimentality" ([both](https://www.metacritic.com/movie/the-wandering-earth/critic-reviews/)).

**All Wishes Come True! (2026).** *New:* the Eight Immortals begin as frauds. Eight powerless mortals take over a
derelict temple, promise to answer every prayer, go viral, and plot to rob the star gods' vault. The design brief is a
pun: 八仙过海 ("the Eight Immortals cross the sea") becomes 瞒天过海 ("deceive heaven to cross the sea"), the first of
the Thirty-Six Stratagems, so an origin myth becomes a heist comedy. *Reused:* the Ming *Journey to the East*, the heist
ensemble, the post-*Ne Zha* myth-remix wave. *Material:* Penglai drawn from a Qing jewelled bonsai in the Palace Museum;
the wish-ribbon tree modelled on Mount Qingcheng's ancient ginkgo; a Sichuan hotpot dinner
([Red Star News](https://static.cdsb.com/micropub/Articles/202607/2afc22a34c3fe09b329e5f9721694308.html)). *Time:*
reversal on reversal for 144 minutes; the complaint was "a bloated runtime and uneven pacing"
([Rotten Tomatoes](https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/all_wishes_come_true)).

**Lord of Mysteries (novel 2018–20; game trailers 2024–26).** *New:* power by acting. Beyonders drink potions named for
roles (Seer, Clown, Faceless) and digest them only by living the role; fail, and they go mad. The hero, a nobody, poses
as a god called The Fool above a grey fog and convenes strangers who know each other only by tarot names: his bluff is
the acting method applied to the whole plot. *Reused:* Lovecraft in a Victorian steampunk city, tarot, web-novel
transmigration. *Material:* occult procedure (pendulums, mirror divination, alchemy); the author's programmer habit of
checking every chapter against a settings file. *Time:* two updates a day for two years; the game trailers return to the
first town as an "old dream". Newcomers to the anime said the lore "flew by fast" ([Screen
Rant](https://screenrant.com/lord-of-mysteries-confusing-story-explained/)).

### Manga and anime

**Attack on Titan (2009–21; anime 2013–23).** *New:* the predator is a vacant copy of us, naked grinning bodies that eat
people without needing to, with humanity kept like livestock inside walls. Then the mystery box changes sides: the
basement opens (chapter 85) on a photograph of a thriving world beyond the sea, and chapter 91 jumps four years into the
enemy's war, where the heroes' island is the threat. *Reused:* *Muv-Luv Alternative*'s man-eating aliens, the shōnen
cadet corps. *Material:* Isayama's mountain-ringed home town ("the society outside the walls, outside the countryside,
has titans in it", [President Online](https://president.jp/articles/-/26931?page=3), translated) and a menacing drunk at
his night job. *Time:* chapter 1, "To You, 2,000 Years From Now", is answered ten years later by chapter 122, "From You,
2,000 Years Ago". Isayama: "the way to make a story interesting is the same: corner the characters" ([Mantan
Web](https://mantan-web.jp/article/20211020dog00m200031000c.html), translated).

**Psycho-Pass (2012).** *New:* the gun that judges. The Dominator reads a target's Crime Coefficient and stays locked
below 100; the officer operates, the gun decides. In episode 11 a man kills a woman in front of the heroine and the gun
won't fire, because his reading is clean. *Reused:* *Minority Report*, *Blade Runner*, the 1980s rookie-and-veteran cop
show. *Material:* a villain who reads Orwell and Gibson; a writer steering away from his own studio's *Ghost in the
Shell* (no cyborgs, no SWAT teams). *Time:* case-of-the-week episodes that each lead back to one man; the finale repeats
the first episode's dialogue almost word for word. Urobuchi: minds turned into numbers make "a paradise, but a
frightening world" ([Anime! Anime!](https://animeanime.jp/article/2012/10/19/11795.html), translated). The complaint:
"too much flat out philosophizing" ([ANN](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/review/psycho-pass/episodes-12)).

**Parasyte (1989–95), Tokyo Ghoul (2011–14) and Ajin (2012–21).** Three manga about a boy turned into what his society
hunts, each with its own new rule. In *Parasyte* a possession fails halfway: the alien lives in Shinichi's right hand,
argues with him and needs him alive, and the title word finally turns out to mean us. Iwaaki wanted to write about the
"egotism of the human race" but "didn't want to look down on humans"
([Kodansha](https://kodansha.us/wp-json/wp/v2/posts?slug=creator-interview-hitoshi-iwaaki-on-parasyte)). In *Tokyo
Ghoul* ghouls can digest only flesh, water and coffee, so the monsters' refuge is a café, and the police fight with
weapons made from dead ghouls' organs. In *Ajin* revival is physics with loopholes: the villain, an old man in a cap,
feeds himself into a crusher so that he regrows inside a guarded tower. *Reused:* body snatchers, vampires, the X-Men
minority. *Material and time:* *Parasyte* grew from childhood food-chain documentaries and breaks quiet desk scenes (a
boy arguing with his hand at night) with one-panel killings; *Tokyo Ghoul* maps its war onto Tokyo's wards; *Ajin*'s
terror arrives in numbered "waves" on a published schedule, like heists.

**Spy × Family (2019–; anime 2022).** *New:* a sitcom made of concealed genres. A spy father, an assassin mother and a
telepathic daughter each hide a secret from the others, and only the child, who reads every mind, knows everything, so
the audience watches from her seat ([ANN](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/review/spy-family/episodes-1-2/.184756)).
World peace depends on a six-year-old's grades, and the fake marriage exists because the school's entrance interview
requires two parents. *Reused:* Cold War spy fiction in a not-quite-divided Germany, *Mr. & Mrs. Smith*, the
found-family sitcom. *Material:* Japan's elite school-entrance culture with its family interview (our reading; Endo
hasn't said so). *Time:* self-contained "missions" that move the big plot in tiny steps, so readers can join anywhere.

**Astro Boy (manga 1952–68; TV 1963–66).** *New:* a child robot who feels, and whose maker rejects him: Dr Tenma
builds him to replace a dead son, then sells him to a circus because he will never grow. The editor's brief was "a
robot that cries, laughs and gets angry for justice" ([Tezuka](https://tezukaosamu.net/en/manga/291.html)). The form
was new too: a story-driven half hour every week for four years, affordable only through limited animation (lip flaps,
held poses, a bank of reusable cels). *Reused:* Pinocchio, Frankenstein, American TV-cartoon economics. *Material:* the
atomic age, six years after Hiroshima: a boy named Atom with a reactor heart. *Time:* it premiered on New Year's
Day 1963 and ended on New Year's Eve 1966, and the shortcuts its budget forced became TV anime's standard method.

### Power, games and screens

**Game of Thrones (2011–19).** *New:* the star is beheaded in episode 9. A big-budget fantasy kills its moral centre,
and from then on nobody is safe. Fantasy is played as an HBO crime drama; the showrunners' rule was that the classic HBO
shows "took established, tired genres and found a way to reinvigorate them" ([Mother
Jones](https://www.motherjones.com/media/2013/03/hbo-game-thrones-season-3-interview-david-benioff-dan-weiss/)).
*Reused:* the Wars of the Roses, Tolkien's maps and dragons. *Material:* the Red Wedding "was stolen from history"
(Scotland's Black Dinner and Glencoe, and the law of hospitality;
[EW](https://web.archive.org/web/2013/http://insidetv.ew.com/2013/06/02/game-of-thrones-author-george-r-r-martin-why-he-wrote-the-red-wedding/2/));
the Wall began on a 1981 visit to Hadrian's Wall: "you take reality and turn it up to eleven"
([Martin](https://georgerrmartin.com/notablog/2019/03/30/watchers-on-hadrians-wall/)). *Time:* talk-heavy episodes build
to a ninth-episode peak; at the Red Wedding the band's song is the fuse, heard before any blade appears.

**House of Cards (2013).** *New:* the delivery system. Thirteen "chapters" released at once, with no pilot: "The
novelty… lies less with its themes than with its delivery system"
([NYT](https://web.archive.org/web/20131102055700/http://tv.nytimes.com/2013/02/01/arts/television/house-of-cards-on-netflix-stars-kevin-spacey.html)).
*Reused:* the BBC original's asides to camera, *Richard III*. *Material:* Beau Willimon's years on campaigns, including
advance work in Iowa. *Time:* before the credits Frank strangles a dying dog and explains himself to the lens, so we are
complicit from the first minute; the season is one thirteen-hour revenge plot. Fincher: "It's like a book… come back,
fire it up again"
([HitFix](https://web.archive.org/web/20130202234010/http://www.hitfix.com/whats-alan-watching/house-of-cards-director-david-fincher-on-making-13-hours-for-netflix)).

**The Hunger Games (2008 novel, 2012 film).** *New:* survival as audience management. The death match already existed;
Collins added a reality show's whole production cycle (reaping, costume parade, host interview, sponsors' silver
parachutes), so the romance is strategy, and the poisoned-berry ending wins by breaking the show's script. *Reused:*
Theseus's tribute of youths, Rome's bread and circuses. *Material:* "channel surfing between reality TV programs and
actual war coverage"
([SLJ](https://web.archive.org/web/2012/http://www.slj.com/2008/09/authors-illustrators/a-killer-story-an-interview-with-suzanne-collins-author-of-the-hunger-games/));
her father, a Vietnam veteran who taught military history at West Point; Appalachian coal country. *Time:* first-person
present tense, and clocks built into the world: a 60-second countdown on the plates, a cannon for each death, the dead
projected on the sky each night.

**Ender's Game (1977 story, 1985 novel) and Ready Player One (2011 novel, 2018 film).** Both make a game the real thing.
*Ender:* "There were no games. The battles were real" ([Card](http://www.hatrack.com/osc/stories/enders-game.shtml)), so
the guilt falls on the adults who lied to a child; the zero-g Battle Room teaches that "the enemy's gate is down". Card
drew on Civil War command histories read as a boy; italic adult voices open each chapter to hide the twist. *Ready
Player One:* nostalgia is the mechanic (a dead billionaire's will makes his 1980s canon the road to power), and the
novel hid a real Easter egg that readers hunted for a DeLorean. Its seed is labour history: an Atari programmer hid his
name in *Adventure* in 1980 because he "was pissed off" at $22K a year with "no royalties"
([Wired](https://www.wired.com/2015/03/warren-robinett-adventure/)). By 2018 the references were the complaint:
"bombarded with images, bored by the lack of an interesting story"
([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/ready-player-one-film-review-steven-spielberg/)). A reference is not an invention.

### Machines and bodies

**Ex Machina (2015).** *New:* the Turing test turned around. Nathan shows the machine openly ("The real test is to show
you she is a robot. Then see if you still feel she has consciousness"), and the test then turns on the tester: Caleb
cuts his own arm to check he's human. Ava's body is plainly machine, but her face, hands and feet are "photographic in
99 percent of the shots", made without a single greenscreen ([The
Verge](https://www.theverge.com/2015/5/8/8572317/ex-machina-movie-visual-effects-interview-robot-ava)). *Reused:*
Frankenstein, Pygmalion, Bluebeard's locked rooms. *Material:* Murray Shanahan's cognitive robotics (the code on screen
prints his book's ISBN); a Norwegian landscape hotel. *Time:* a one-week clock; red blackouts as the only privacy; one
burst of tempo, the drilled disco dance.

**Alita: Battle Angel (2019).** *New:* a manga face made photoreal, acting opposite live actors. When the first
trailer's oversized eyes were mocked, Cameron made them bigger, not smaller
([Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/eye-popping-secrets-alita-battle-angel-including-james-cameron-wanted-big-eyes-210836310.html)).
*Reused:* Pinocchio, Frankenstein, *Rollerball*, the doomed teen romance. *Material:* pro aggressive inline skaters for
street Motorball; Panama City, Havana and Guanajuato's tunnels for Iron City ([SYFY
Wire](https://www.syfy.com/syfy-wire/why-alita-battle-angel-moved-from-japan-to-latin-america-and-used-so-many-3d-printers)).
*Time:* her past returns as glitches, so her skill arrives before her story. NPR found the "billiard-ball sized eyes"
stopped distracting "after a few minutes"
([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2019/02/14/694067590/teenage-wasteland-alita-battle-angel-doesn-t-quite-earn-its-wings)).

**Big Hero 6 (2014).** *New:* the superhero robot is an inflatable nurse whose catchphrase is the clinic's pain scale,
and the team origin runs on grief, not power. *Reused:* Marvel team assembly, a boy and his creature. *Material:* Don
Hall saw an inflatable care arm in Chris Atkeson's soft-robotics lab at Carnegie Mellon, "our ticket to putting a robot
on the screen we had never seen before"
([CMU](https://www.cmu.edu/news/stories/archives/2014/october/october29_baymax.html)); a child grief counsellor's method
(ask questions until the child finds the answer) became Baymax's
([IndieWire](https://www.indiewire.com/features/general/immersed-in-movies-don-hall-and-chris-williams-talk-tadashi-is-here-from-big-hero-6-123729/)).
*Time:* the dead brother's video is planted early, cut off, and played in full at the low point.

**Beyond the Aquila Rift (Love, Death & Robots, 2019).** *New:* photorealism as bait. A game-trailer studio makes the
most lifelike things on screen (the lover, the warm station, the love scene) the lie. Asking for the truth, Thom finds
himself still in his sleep tank inside a web of wrecked ships, and "Greta" is a many-eyed creature that wipes his memory
and restarts the illusion. *Reused:* *Alien*'s hypersleep, *Solaris*'s rebuilt lover, the *Matrix* pod. *Material:*
Alastair Reynolds was an astronomer at the European Space Agency, and the Aquila Rift is a real band of dark clouds.
*Time:* the truth arrives in rising doses (a detour, then 150,000 light-years and centuries, then a false world), and
the end loops. Reynolds praised the "perspective shift"
([blog](https://approachingpavonis.blogspot.com/2019/03/love-death-robots.html)); critics flagged waxy eyes and the male
gaze.

### The mainstream heart

**Frozen (2013).** *New:* Disney fakes out its own true-love's-kiss twice, pays it off with a sister's sacrifice, and
makes the love-at-first-sight prince the villain. A song rewrote the film: after "Let It Go", Jennifer Lee turned Elsa
from villain to heroine and "rewrote the whole first act" ([Den of
Geek](https://www.denofgeek.com/movies/chris-buck-and-jennifer-lee-interview-on-making-frozen/)). *Reused:* *The Snow
Queen*, the Broadway-style Disney musical, a comic sidekick. *Material:* Frode Fjellheim's Sámi joik "Vuelie"; fjords,
stave churches and rosemaling from a research trip to Norway; an opening work song for ice harvesters sawing a lake.
*Time:* one song crosses years of childhood at a closed door.

**Joker (2019).** *New:* a comic-book villain's origin as a 1970s New York character study, with no hero and no set
pieces, and the laugh recast as a condition Arthur apologises for on a printed card. *Reused:* openly, *Taxi Driver* and
*The King of Comedy*; IndieWire said Scorsese "probably deserves an executive producer credit"
([IndieWire](https://www.indiewire.com/2019/08/joker-review-joaquin-phoenix-1202170236/)). *Material:* Carson-era
late-night TV; Hildur Guðnadóttir's feedback cello. *Time:* her music was written from the script and played on set, and
Phoenix's improvised bathroom dance to it replaced a scripted scene
([IndieWire](https://www.indiewire.com/2019/10/joker-bathroom-dance-todd-phillips-cut-scene-1202180584/)); the Bronx
stairs are a clock, climbed in defeat and danced down at the end; long humiliation, then sudden violence.

**Last Christmas (2019).** *New:* the twist hides in the title lyric (Tom is the donor of Kate's transplanted heart, so
"I gave you my heart" is literal), inside a Christmas romantic comedy about a family of Yugoslav refugees in
Brexit-era London. *Reused:* *Love Actually*'s London, the dead-all-along twist, *A Christmas Carol*. *Material:* George
Michael's songbook, deep cuts included; the ex-Yugoslav diaspora. *Time:* clues in plain sight (Tom never interacts
with anyone else). Variety didn't know "whether to swoon or collapse into vaguely annoyed giggles"
([Variety](https://variety.com/2019/film/reviews/last-christmas-review-emilia-clarke-henry-golding-1203391871/)). The
lesson is its nerve: one literalised metaphor carries the whole design.

**Superman (2025).** *New:* a film universe launched with no origin story. Title cards end on "Superman lost a battle
for the very first time" three minutes ago, and the parents' sacred message turns out to order conquest, so choice beats
blood. Earnestness is the provocation. *Reused:* the 1978 theme, the Daily Planet romance, Silver Age oddities.
*Material:* Krypto is Gunn's destructive rescue dog
([Variety](https://variety.com/2024/film/news/krypto-superman-james-gunn-superdog-1236178707/)); Lois was built from
talks with working reporters. *Time:* relentless, with one long pause (an interview in Lois's flat); his childhood
appears only at the end, as home movies. NPR: "corny. Hokey. Cheesy. Achingly sincere. Cringe, even. Which is to say:
It's Superman" ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2025/07/10/nx-s1-5453867/superman-review)).

### Days and years

**Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 (2025).** *New:* the Gommage. Each year the Paintress paints a lower number and everyone
of that age is erased, so a whole society lives under a visible, falling countdown, and each expedition is the next
cohort to die. Timed parries inside turn-based combat. The lead dies at the end of Act I, and the world turns out to be
a painting made by a grieving family of painters. Director Guillaume Broche wanted a hook that, "expliqué en deux
phrases, intrigue les gens" ("explained in two sentences, intrigues people";
[Gamekult](https://www.gamekult.com/actualite/interview-clair-obscur-expedition-33-on-ne-s-attendait-pas-a-une-reception-aussi-enorme-3050859619.html)).
*Reused:* *Final Fantasy*, *Paper Mario*'s timed hits. *Material:* Alain Damasio's novel *La Horde du Contrevent*; the
Belle Époque, chosen because games had under-used it. *Time:* a year is the unit, and enemy turns become rhythm. The
complaint: a "seriously fumbled ending"
([Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/games/2025/apr/23/clair-obscur-expedition-33-review-pc-playstation-5)).

**Minecraft (2009–11) and Stardew Valley (2016).** Both are about the rhythm of days. *Minecraft:* a world made entirely
of material, infinite and seeded, sold unfinished with weekly public updates (*Infiniminer* supplied the look). A
20-minute day: "In the brief daytime, you build. In the long night-time, you hide"
([Eurogamer](https://www.eurogamer.net/minecraft-review)). C418's music arrives unannounced after long silence: "the
pause in between actually helps" ([RBMA](https://daily.redbullmusicacademy.com/2015/08/c418-interview/)). *Stardew
Valley:* a *Harvest Moon* in which every system feeds another, the villain is a corporation, and the moral choice is a
mechanic (restore the community centre with gifts, or buy the same upgrades from JojaMart). The opening echoes Eric
Barone's own life (a graduate who didn't want a cubicle and worked as a theatre usher): you read your dead grandfather's
letter at a Joja cubicle and quit. A day lasts about 14 real minutes, a season exactly 28 days, and festivals stop the
clock.

## 2. Principles

Distilled from §1. Each is a question the concept must answer, with the works that answer it best.

1. **A new rule, not a new topic.** Every loved work can be stated as one rule that generates scenes by itself: each
   year a painted number erases everyone that age (*Expedition 33*); the gun won't fire on a clean reading
   (*Psycho-Pass*); ghouls can digest only flesh, water and coffee (*Tokyo Ghoul*). Everyone shares the topics (AI,
   war, family); a rule belongs to one film. A topic plus a mood is not yet a concept.

2. **The form enacts the idea.** The telling does what the story is about. *Arrival*'s flash-forwards make us see as
   the aliens do; *Eternal Sunshine* runs backwards because it is an erasure; Frank's asides in *House of Cards* make
   us his accomplices. Retell the story straight: if nothing is lost, there is no formal invention.

3. **An old scaffold with one load-bearing change.** The loved works borrow a whole genre on purpose and replace one
   beam: the spy sitcom where only the child knows everything (*Spy × Family*), *Taxi Driver* in a comic-book world
   (*Joker*), the death match run as a TV production (*The Hunger Games*), true love's act paid off by a sister
   (*Frozen*). *Game of Thrones*' makers set out to take "established, tired genres" and reinvigorate them. The
   scaffold buys trust; the change is the film.

4. **Material from outside film.** Freshness comes in from the world, not from other films or from trend pieces: Dust
   Bowl testimony (*Interstellar*), dune stabilisation in Oregon (*Dune*), a soft-robotics lab and a grief counsellor's
   method (*Big Hero 6*), a Scottish massacre at a dinner table (*Game of Thrones*), a jewelled bonsai in the Palace
   Museum (*All Wishes Come True!*). The test of a detail is that a writer couldn't have guessed it.

5. **Put the cost inside the rule, and make someone choose.** The best rules charge a personal price and force a choice
   nobody else can make: 23 years of his children's lives for a few hours on one planet (*Interstellar*), the girl or
   the city (*Weathering with You*), a game that was a war (*Ender's Game*). The theme is argued by the choice, never
   said aloud (ANY-2).

6. **Let the second half re-read the first.** The turn changes the meaning of what we've already seen: the basement
   and the jump to the enemy's side (*Attack on Titan*), the chosen one revoked (*Blade Runner 2049*), the prince as
   villain (*Frozen*). A turn that adds meaning is the goal; one that erases it ("none of it was real", MIND-3, MIND-4)
   is banned. *Beyond the Aquila Rift*'s illusion twist survives because its photoreal finish is itself the lie; the
   twist alone would now fail.

7. **Carry scale on one small relationship and one object.** Epic premises ride on a pair and a thing: a father, a
   daughter and a bookshelf (*Interstellar*); two strangers and a name written on a palm (*Your Name*); a boy and his
   own right hand (*Parasyte*); a boy and an inflatable nurse (*Big Hero 6*). The relationship is the stakes, not a
   hidden explanation (ANY-1).

8. **Give time a body you can see or hear.** The loved works put their clocks on screen: the number on the monolith
   (*Expedition 33*), a countdown in the hero's eyes (*The Three-Body Problem*), a cannon for each death and faces in
   the night sky (*The Hunger Games*), the stairs climbed and then danced down (*Joker*), the launch countdown over a
   farewell (*Interstellar*), a 28-day season (*Stardew Valley*). A visible clock makes a short film feel large.

9. **Contrast makes tempo.** Speed reads only after stillness, and silence only after noise. One speed has a cost:
   slowness was the main complaint against *Blade Runner 2049*, and *Tenet* sprints so hard that its exposition drowns.
   The works that switch feel alive: *Your Name*'s music-video montage, then a dead stop at its midpoint; one long
   conversation inside relentless *Superman*; one burst of dancing inside still *Ex Machina*; Minecraft's music after
   long silence, since "the pause in between actually helps". Set all four dials of the tempo map (playbook §1), not
   just cut length.

10. **Make at least one choice someone will complain about.** Freshness shows up at release as a complaint about a
    deliberate choice: the eyes made bigger (*Alita*), the "selfish" ending (*Weathering with You*), the literal
    lyric (*Last Christmas*), the length (*Blade Runner 2049*), the buried dialogue (*Tenet*). A concept nobody could
    object to is the median. The complaint must be about a choice, never about carelessness.

11. **Turn the medium's limits into the idea.** Constraints became signatures: film magazines set *The Odyssey*'s
    rhythm, a weekly TV budget made *Astro Boy*'s shortcuts the standard method of TV anime, and a trailer studio's
    photorealism became *Aquila Rift*'s lie. Our medium is generated stills and short generated shots. Its stillness
    and drift are material to design with (MADE-2, MADE-4), never defaults to hide.

12. **Name the new thing.** A new core usually needs a coined noun or a real term the audience has never heard: the
    Gommage, the Dominator, inversion, the Acting Method, the Battle Room, Earth Engines, *kuchikamizake*. If the
    concept needs no new word, check whether it is new.

## 4. Sourcing specific human material

Material comes from the people who do the thing, in their own words, before anyone summarised it. Explainers,
listicles, viral posts, trend reports and a model's first answer all sit downstream of that, and downstream is where
the median lives. Miyazaki's complaint about his own industry applies to us: work "produced by humans who can't stand
looking at other humans" ([Wikiquote](https://en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Hayao_Miyazaki)).

### Method
1. **Start from a practice, not a topic.** Write the seed as one sentence naming who, where, which trade, which object
   and which year. If you can't name a trade, a place or an object, you still have a theme, not material. *Parasite*
   began with a tutoring job: "I got this feeling that I was infiltrating the private lives of complete strangers"
   (Bong Joon-ho, [via Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parasite_(2019_film))).
2. **Read primary sources first.** Each of these yields first-person detail that no summary carries:
   - *Oral history:* the Library of Congress's [Occupational Folklife
     Project](https://www.loc.gov/collections/occupational-folklife-project) (2,000+ interviews across 100+ trades); the
     [Computer History Museum](https://computerhistory.org/oral-histories/) interviews (tech founders before their myths
     set); Mass Observation diaries of ordinary days. British Library Sounds has been offline since the 2023
     cyber-attack.
   - *Records of things going wrong:* NASA's [ASRS](https://asrs.arc.nasa.gov/), where pilots, controllers and mechanics
     confess their own errors; NTSB dockets; OSHA fatality summaries; English coroners'
     [prevention-of-future-deaths reports](https://www.judiciary.uk/prevention-of-future-death-reports/); the one-line
     emergency-room narratives of [NEISS](https://www.cpsc.gov/Research--Statistics/NEISS-Injury-Data).
   - *Courts and print:* the [Old Bailey](https://www.oldbaileyonline.org/) (197,000 trials, 1674–1913), CourtListener,
     BAILII; HathiTrust full-text search, trade papers at World Radio History, the "background" sections of patents.
   - *Practitioners:* PPRuNe (pilots), Practical Machinist, Eng-Tips, The Farming Forum, r/AskHistorians. Read the
     low-upvote working threads; the viral posts are already tropes. Save copies, because Reddit has blocked the
     Wayback Machine since August 2025.
3. **Regional Chinese life.** Download what you use; Chinese sources disappear.
   - *Court judgments:* the facts paragraphs (经审理查明) of civil cases on 中国裁判文书网: bride-price refunds, loans between
     relatives, work injuries, homestead disputes. It needs a login and is shrinking: uploads fell from 19.2 million in
     2020 to 5.11 million in 2023
     ([Pekingnology](https://www.pekingnology.com/p/chinas-highest-court-defends-restricting)), and over 11 million
     cases were removed in 2021 ([MIT Technology
     Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2023/12/20/1085741/china-judgements-online-transparency-government/)).
   - *Gazetteers and yearbooks:* county 志 and 年鉴 record floods, factories, prices and model workers year by year, the
     dull official record where odd facts hide. [LoGaRT](https://logart.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/) searches Ming–Qing
     gazetteers in full text; CNKI's yearbooks are closed to institutions outside the mainland since 2023.
   - *First-person non-fiction:* [网易人间](https://renjian.163.com/) (active, pseudonymous accounts), 正午故事,
     三联生活周刊, the National Library's [中国记忆](https://memory.nlc.cn/) interviews with craftspeople, and
     [404文库](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/404-archive/) for deleted first-person posts (use the posts, not the
     site's framing). The status of 真实故事计划, 谷雨 and 澎湃镜相 is unverified.
   - *Platforms:* 快手, whose early growth came from rural life filmed by farmers, drivers, fishers and factory hands;
     B站 user documentaries about hometowns and trades; 知乎 "what is it like to be X" answers; 豆瓣小组 serial
     diaries; trade 贴吧 (posts before 2017 are hidden). 小红书 is the most trend-driven and the likeliest to hand you
     a cliché.
   - *Method models:* Liang Hong followed about fifty of her village's migrant workers to their cities (《出梁庄记》);
     Wang Bing lived near Zhili's garment workshops for years and waited three months for access before shooting
     *Youth (Spring)* ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youth_(Spring))). Access is earned with time.
4. **Collect unguessable details.** For each concept, keep at least five details a writer could not have invented:
   jargon, a tool, a number, a ritual, a joke, a way things fail. Ask what people felt and remembered, not only what
   happened: Alexievich writes "a history of human feelings" ([alexievich.info](http://alexievich.info/en/)). The
   richest find is what the speaker didn't know they knew. A woman hearing her own interview played back told
   Studs Terkel: "I never knew I felt that way before" ([Wikiquote](https://en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Studs_Terkel)).
5. **Transform before use.** The material shapes the world's rules, the way Herbert's dune reporting became Arrakis's
   ecology; it is not dramatised as itself. Change every name, place and date, and never let a private person's story
   be recognisable. Raw copies go in `work/<episode>/sources/`, which is gitignored; committed files name public
   sources only.

### Is the material itself already cliché?
1. **Distance.** Search the core noun phrase in English and Chinese on YouTube and B站, IMDb keywords and 豆瓣 tags,
   Letterboxd lists, Short of the Week and TV Tropes. If the image fills the first page of results or appears in three
   or more shorts, it's taken: demote it to texture.
2. **Peak.** Check Google Trends, 百度指数 and 巨量算数. A spike in the last twelve months means dozens of filmmakers are
   on it now; go narrower (one trade, one county, one year) or elsewhere.
3. **Provenance.** If you first met the detail in an explainer, a viral post or a model's answer, it is circulating.
   Trace it to a primary source or drop it.
4. **Official fame.** Anything on a national heritage list ([中国非物质文化遗产网](https://www.ihchina.cn/), UNESCO) is
   already famous and probably filmed. Look for the unlisted practice next to it.
5. **Practitioner.** Show it to someone in the trade, or read their forum. "Everyone knows that" means authentic
   texture, not a story. "Nobody ever writes about this" means keep it.
6. **Median.** Ask a fresh model session with no context for ten film ideas on the theme. Anything on that list is
   the median, and it is banned for this episode.

## 5. The concept test

Every concept passes this test before anything is written. The concept session answers the questions; a separate
judge session scores the answers, and it runs its own searches rather than trusting the writer's.

### What the concept document must answer
0. **The median list.** Before pitching, list the 20 most predictable takes on this episode's themes, including
   whatever a fresh model session suggests for "ten short films about X". These are banned for this episode on top of
   §3.
1. **Is the core new, or only the surface?** State the core in one sentence with no setting, style or names: the rule,
   and the choice it forces. Name the three nearest existing works (film, series, short, game, novel, viral AI short)
   and how the core differs from each. Name the new noun (principle 12), if there is one.
2. **What is its formal invention?** Say what the telling does that a plain retelling would lose (principle 2).
3. **Which banned takes does it touch, and are they core or texture?** List every §3 ID and median-list item it
   touches, each marked core or texture as §3 defines them.
4. **Where is its specific human material from?** Give the sources (§4) and at least five unguessable details that
   will be on screen.
5. **Does its tempo map have real contrast?** Give the four dials per section (cut length, movement in the shot,
   sound and voice density, intensity), with at least one acceleration, one hard stop and one stillness or silence
   (playbook §1), and the clock the audience can see or hear (principle 8).
6. **Can a stranger retell it?** Two sentences that make someone want to see it; Broche's test for *Expedition 33* was
   a hook that, "explained in two sentences, intrigues people". Also name the deliberate choice someone will complain
   about (principle 10).

### Rubric
Score each criterion 0–5. Anchors are given for 0, 1, 3 and 5; use 2 and 4 for concepts between them.

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Core** | a §3 or median-list take | a known core in a new setting or style | an old scaffold with one load-bearing change no one has made | a new rule that generates scenes by itself and needs a new word |
| **Form** | nothing lost if told plainly | a look or a style only | a device that carries meaning across several scenes | the form enacts the idea; it can't be told another way |
| **Banned** | a banned take is core (the gate fails) | five or more as texture: a collage of familiar moves | three or four as texture | none touched (4: one or two, transformed) |
| **Material** | trend summaries, general knowledge | a named subculture with invented details | a real practice with sourced jargon, tools and numbers | primary sources few have read, built into the world's rules, 5+ unguessable details on screen |
| **Tempo** | one tempo throughout | only cut length varies | all four dials vary, with an acceleration, a hard stop and a stillness | time is part of the form: a visible clock or time device shapes the whole |
| **Pull** | can't be said in two sentences | a premise, but no stake | a clear hook and a poster image | a stranger retells it after one hearing and wants to see it |

**Verdict.** *Drop* if the gate fails, any criterion scores 0 or 1, or the total is under 18 out of 30. Otherwise
*pass* if every criterion is 3 or more and the total is 22 or more. Everything else is *revise*: one round with the
judge's notes, then a fresh score. A merged concept is scored again from scratch.

**Calibration.** Score these two before judging, to set the scale.
- *Arrival*, judged as a 2016 pitch: Core 5 (a language that changes how its learner perceives time), Form 5 (the
  flash-forward read as a flashback), Banned 4 (the military standoff and global countdown are texture), Material 4
  (field linguistics with a working linguist), Tempo 4 (patient sessions inside a speeding clock, a looping score),
  Pull 4. Total 26: pass.
- *CONTINUITY*, our pilot: Core 1 (a dead leader kept broadcasting by a machine), Form 3 (the frozen image mirrors the
  frozen regime, and the autocomplete carries the thesis), Banned gate fails (POWER-1 and POWER-8 are its core, and
  MADE-6 its angle), Material 1 (an invented technician, no primary source), Tempo 1 (cut lengths varied; nothing else
  did), Pull 3. Drop. What to keep from it is its instinct to make form out of the medium's limits (principle 11).

   merge it with the writer's.
2. For each concept, run the §4 distance and peak checks on the core sentence, and add any near work the writer
   missed.
3. Write one verdict per concept in the JSON below, as a list in `episodes/<episode>/concept_verdicts.json`, and
   validate it with `python3 -m json.tool`.
4. Pick or merge, re-score any merge, and only then write `episode.md`.
