# 第一天 — choreography

The dance is a conversation that eventually stops needing the cut. It belongs to two attractive adults, neither a singer impersonation nor an anonymous perfect ensemble. Lin is relaxed and precise; Yu is precise but replies a fraction too early. Their faces remain composed while their bodies carry the exuberance.

## One phrase, three meanings

**Grounded heel touch → quarter turn → open palm → one backward step.** A relaxed spring in the knees connects the events. Keep the footwork compact, the shoulders free and the hand arc clear. Contemporary partnering with lightly buoyant social-dance footwork is the base; avoid turning the brief into a catalogue of dance styles.

Use an eight-count development phrase if useful in planning: touch, recover, turn, settle, offer, allow the offer to register, retreat, settle. These counts are a rehearsal description, **not a measured beat map**. Fit durations to the actual supplied recording; LRC syllable starts do not establish beat onsets. No recording time-stretching.

This separates readable social hesitation from implausible transport choreography.

## Contact and the final turn

Use a single uncomplicated open hand connection: Lin offers her right hand and Yu receives with his left, provided the approved staging keeps both wrists and faces readable. Lock that choice in the shot reference; do not change it invisibly between angles. Hands approach separately before contact. No finger interlacing is needed.

For the partner turn, begin with feet supported, arms comfortably bent and the pair at a natural distance. One guides a small quarter/half turn while the other travels a short arc; both finish balanced. Keep the hands away from the face. The purpose is shared direction and timing. An overhead lift, dip, aerial exchange or deep counterbalance would add risk without strengthening this story.

Save an uninterrupted full-body phrase for the first part of the final chorus. Later close details can celebrate it after the shared floor and actual contact are established. The camera must not orbit while the bodies perform the most important turn; one source of complex motion is enough for this beat.

## Between dancing

The second verse has no full display phrase. Yu adjusts wind-disturbed hair or traps a jacket edge under his leg; Lin privately tries one fragment of his too-early retreat. Looking at the same sea and returning an eye-line are complete actions. Do not fill every musical accent with another gesture.

At 164 seconds the body and camera density drop sharply. Lin is already entirely on I, feet planted, arms resting. A departs behind her. Her stillness is chosen; no freeze-frame effect or exaggerated performance explains it. The recording continues. When Yu sees the empty space beside her, his subsequent crossing supplies the reply.

## Keyframes and eventual motion

Produce balanced anticipatory poses: clear support foot, free heel near the floor, separated arms, unobscured hand, face visible. Avoid a keyframe with crossed shins, airborne feet, an obscured supporting knee or a wrist hidden behind another wrist. A graceful silhouette that cannot plausibly bear weight is not approved.

For a meaningful phrase, define **start, required event, end**, not a long string of emotion words. A crossing needs an open level dock at the start and both feet on I at the end. The waiting beat needs Lin on I and A behind her before departure. Contact needs separate hands before the touch. The final turn needs a settled finish.

The current output is a pre-motion reel: hold the approved poses and make selected hard pose cuts. Do not claim footwork, weight, timing or hand contact are verified. Later video tests should begin with the final shared turn because it is the hardest load-bearing action. In each full take record when the required event actually happens, then ensure the final selected cut retains it. If motion is late, use the usable interval; if absent, still-frame plausibility is not a substitute. Playback review remains necessary.
