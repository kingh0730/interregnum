# 第一天 — cinematography

Photographic, full 16:9. The camera is a companion standing near the two people, learning where the other is by watching their attention. It is not a promotional drone touring the city. Show the spectacular world when it explains their distance or enlarges their eventual togetherness.

## Home and departures

The home register is lateral, partner-height observation on 35/50 mm lenses. Use a 24/28 mm lens for the establishing geography and an occasional scale reveal. A 65/85 mm detail belongs to a particular hand, shoe or noticed object. A lens number is a staging instruction, not a guarantee of generated optical behaviour; judge the resulting perspective.

For ordinary two-person spatial coverage, the common camera stands north and faces the sea to the south. Lin lives screen left, looking toward screen right; Yu lives screen right, looking toward screen left. Early match cuts must preserve both that relationship and recognizable set anchors. A reverse angle is permitted after a wider view re-establishes it. Do not silently mirror a frame: the ear cuff, docks and landmarks would reverse.

Each eventual camera move earns a purpose: a lateral follow reveals an approaching dock; one elevated view explains the offset routes; a rising final wide releases the pair into the wider city. The hard stop at 164 seconds interrupts this moving home register. Keep the held startframes held in the story reel; future motion instructions do not authorize automatic digital pans or push-ins now.

| Waiting | 35/50 mm, locked camera, Lin beside a deliberate empty area | Let absence last without exaggerated facial acting |

For dance coverage, target roughly 60–75% frame height for the standing bodies where the lens and architecture permit. Leave travel room above and to each side. Keep heads, fingertips and both shoes inside the frame, with enough lower margin that screen-space lyrics can be placed without obscuring footwork. These are framing aims, not a reason to crop a good performance mechanically.

Depth normally has a small foreground edge, the subject, and a separated architectural/light plane. Avoid a railing across a knee, wrist or face merely to satisfy that pattern. A support rib at one edge is enough. Environment views may use deeper focus; close details may fall away naturally. Neither razor-sharp pores nor universal background blur is the look.

One large source makes the light legible. Screen direction is set by the approved location master and changes correctly on reverse angles. Do not copy the phrase “sun from screen left” to every shot irrespective of view. Palette variations arise from shade, exposed stone and sea reflections within one selected art direction.

## Things the cut must establish

Three clear visual facts outrank attractive inserts: **initial separation; Lin remains on I while A departs; Yu sees her and joins through the level right dock**. Keep a face/eyeline link in the last sequence so his crossing reads as an answer. Before 160 seconds I is empty; the earlier failures are hesitations aboard.

The final duet needs at least one eventual take in which two people complete the familiar phrase, with contact and feet visible. A hand close-up followed by a beautiful two-shot cannot by itself prove that action. Mark it pending until full-take and final-cut playback checks verify it. In this reel, pose cuts illustrate the action and remain visibly a story-reel technique.

Inspect starting frames in sequence for face identity, silhouette, limb separation, costume, dock state, screen direction, light, horizon and set anchors. At reduced viewing size the pair must remain distinguishable; at full resolution their hands and faces must remain plausible. Do not assign a motion-quality pass from those still checks.
