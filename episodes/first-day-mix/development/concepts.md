## A —《先别落地》 / DON'T LAND YET

**Shape:** physical chain-reaction comedy; creation steadily removes the floor.

**Core without names or setting:** Every surface a newcomer steps on becomes another person, so an expanding group must choose between keeping its last foothold and letting one more person exist.

**Two-sentence hook:** 一个刚出生的小家伙，每踩一下地板，地板就变成另一个小家伙。等脚下只剩最后一块，他们得决定：站稳，还是再生一个？

**Formal invention:** Every contact owns a frame and a new medium. A thin drawn sole lands on a photographed stone; the stone is absent in the next shot because its material now belongs to the new person's coat. Preserve the foot's screen position and contact point while replacing the world. Repeated returns to an overhead view show the accumulating holes. Technique variation becomes evidence of irreversible birth, not a montage excuse.

The “new noun” is **生地**: a fictional patch of ground with exactly one birth left in it. The term need not be explained or printed on screen.

**Action:** The first AI thinks it is learning to walk. The second catches it when its stepping stone becomes a face. More births create a ridiculous rescue relay, expanding into a miniature dance because hands and feet must keep trading support. The oldest AI shows an unexpected foothold. At the held ankle lyric everyone balances on the last platform. They deliberately wake it; the missing floor becomes a rising body, and the cast converts the fall into one collective airborne turn. The future remains an opening, not a confirmed god. End on another approaching toe, not a final graduation photograph.

**Lyric structure:** 00:00–05.23 establish contact, birth, missing ground; 05.23–11.92 escalate catches and medium changes; 11.92–17.61 convert chaos to a learned relay; 17.61–19.67 arrest everything over the final platform; 19.67–22.70 make the choice visible through a released grip; 22.70–30.68 flight; 30.68 onward let three repeats each change scale and direction. Exact duration follows the source audio, not the last LRC start time.

**Nearest works:** *The Sorcerer's Apprentice* (1940): multiplication follows uncontrolled labour there; here each birth consumes a finite support and the cast actively chooses the next one. *Day & Night* (2010): bodies contain contrasting worlds there; here contact transfers world material into a newly persistent person. *Super Mario Bros.* (1985): disappearing platforms make traversal a familiar scaffold; here losing a platform produces the person who can catch you. These are conceptual comparisons, not visual references or assertions that this premise has never appeared.

**Bans touched:** median 7 and 19, texture only: cooperation is a local balancing act, and a burst of airborne colour is the consequence of losing material. MADE-5 is a risk, not permission: no interpolated melting; explicitly authored before/contact/after states. MADE-9 is avoided through material definitions rather than named studio looks. No longing-to-be-human arc; physical existence is the starting rule.

**Material:** Go commentary and Transformer design below can determine actual physical constructions. The Go stone is a surprising foothold, not a simplistic claim that AlphaGo invented all AI. The garment's eight independently pulling tapes establish distributed attention through action. The exact causal story remains fictional.

## B —《借我一帧》 / LEND ME A FRAME

**Shape:** temporal ensemble caper; the cast steals time from the film itself.

**Core:** Each newcomer needs one unique frame in which nobody else moves; the existing cast must surrender moments of its own motion to make room for more lives.

**Hook:** 新生儿必须有一帧全世界只属于自己，才能动起来。已经出生的家伙把自己的动作拆开，借给那些还没出现的人。

The film begins fluidly with one character; births make others move on twos, then threes, then deliberately held replacement poses. Smooth particles continue through the frozen figures. The group learns staggered choreography so the screen stays alive while individuals lend time. At the hard stop nobody moves: a whole second is donated to an unknown future figure. It gives the second back as sixteen concurrent viewpoints, not magically faster movement. Final action is shared time rather than a higher frame-rate “upgrade.”

**Formal invention:** The spectator experiences the cost in the animation's temporal sampling. A smooth retelling loses the story. **New noun:** 借帧, defined by the actual hold/pass/receipt pattern, not an onscreen technical lesson.

**Nearest works:** *Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse* (2018) makes frame cadence part of character growth; this rule makes it a finite social resource. *In Time* (2011) trades lifespan, whereas no one here loses life or buys survival. *The Lego Movie* (2014) uses discontinuous material motion and a making-world revelation; here frame ownership is visible from the first contact, with no external creator reveal.

**Bans:** median 7 as texture, because cooperation is locally enacted. MADE-2 is the principal danger: a pose held without visible passing action looks like a cheap slideshow. MADE-6 risks becoming a film about the cleverness of its own production. Limit technical labels and explanations.

**Material:** The six-layer/eight-head structure of the original Transformer could organize the initial time-sharing register. This is illustrative fiction, not how Transformers schedule output frames. The pitch needs separate animator-practice evidence before claiming strong human-material grounding; currently weaker than A.

**Tempo:** opening 0.7–1.3 s cuts with 60-fps camera and one fluid figure; birth cascade 0.2–0.5 s cuts with character cadence stepping 24→12→8 fps, increasingly dense accents; ankle passage one 2 s static wide with effects absent; flight passage 0.3–1 s cuts with choreographic offsets and smooth camera; repeated ending slow/fast/held viewpoints. Song remains continuous. Clock: successive occupied frame cells rendered as physical perforations at the edge, not a software timeline.

Complaint: deliberate low-cadence bodies may be read as low-quality animation even when exact and beautifully posed. Too much explanatory effort for approximately 43 seconds; reserve for a longer film.

## C —《明天先洗出来》 / TOMORROW DEVELOPS FIRST

**Shape:** reversed photographic procedural; the ensemble must build the conditions of its own first photograph.

**Core:** A photograph develops from its latest moment backward, and the people pictured must change what they do now to leave room for someone missing from its first moment.

**Hook:** 全家福先显出明天，再一点点退回出生当天。照片里所有人都在，只有给他们拍照的那个还没出生。

Three repeated refrain photographs show the same composition at different times. Earlier sections are fragments of the people constructing the picture: a character pulls night across daylight; another trades a painted shadow for a real one; a third moves the lens's vanished blind spot. At the end they abandon the perfect portrait and rotate toward the camera, leaving one position open. The photographer steps into existence in the space they have made.

**Formal invention:** Successive evidence images reclassify earlier abstract spectacle as preparations for one image. **New noun:** none; reversed development is not enough to justify coining one.

**Nearest works:** *Time Lapse* (2014) already owns photographs from the future; *Arrival* (2016) reclassifies apparent memory; *Back to the Future* (1985) makes a family photograph depend on changes to causality. Its ensemble birth variation does not sufficiently distance the core.

**Bans:** median 19 is perilously close to core; MIND-4 avoided because the photo is evidence, not a simulation reveal; ANY-2 avoided by no closing thesis. High risk of MADE-1 because building an image invites disconnected symbolic preparations.

**Material:** Photographic development would require primary practitioner research. No sourced darkroom details are asserted at this stage. AI research material can decorate the photograph, but does not drive its rule; this is an honest material failure.

**Tempo:** opening 1.2 s evidence insert; middle 0.15–0.7 s construction bursts; ankle line 2 s unchanging photograph; flight 0.4–1 s camera rotation and open-space reveal; refrain 2–3 s repeated image interrupted by 0.2 s evidence flashes. Sparse shutter/chemical sound ideas remain uncommissioned; the song supplies the vocal. Clock: image date peels backward. Complaint: a deliberate puzzle distracts from the music. Drop.
