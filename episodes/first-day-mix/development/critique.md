# Development critique — 43.670-second music pilot

Reviewed 2026-10-02: `episode.md`, the available art-direction, cinematography, production-design and sound bibles, `research/culture-and-facts.md`, and `development/concepts.md` in this episode folder. This is a separate critical pass by **the same agent that drafted the three concepts**, not blind or independent authorship. No generated motion or sound playback was reviewed. No generation was requested.

**Verdict: keep the selected physical premise, repair the rules before image continuity is locked.** The present formulation cannot literally happen as written. “Every surface touched becomes a person” makes clothing, already-awake bodies, stones and rescue slings infinitely reproductive; it also forbids the cast from holding the final platform until its scheduled awakening. The remedy is a visible, bounded affordance, not an explanatory caption.

## 1. Replace the universal rule with one visible trigger

Recommended treatment sentence:

> A sleeping floor-tile wakes when someone presses its pale footprint. The tile becomes one person, leaving an empty socket; everything already awake can be touched safely.

Each sleeping tile has a pale footprint and **one closed seam**. Activation requires pressing that marked face; gripping a rim, touching its underside, brushing clothing or passing a hand across ordinary scenery does nothing. After activation the footprint is gone. This is the sole exception vocabulary; do not add numerical energy levels, runes or interface panels.

Teach the rule in the first two births, each as **press → figure rises → matching hole remains**. The second event is the proof. Later cut on contact only after the viewer knows what is changing. A medium switch can reveal the same activation from a different representation, but must not look like several births.

The first character can begin already awake, with its empty socket in the opening wide. No need to answer who activated it: the film enters an ongoing physical situation, as the song does. Show DeepSeek's birth in full. The first spoken lyric does not require literally showing every birth.

## 2. Count supports and people once

The current opening contains only two Go stones, but subsequently supplies sixteen product people and a future companion. No finite supply is established. Suggested economical repair:

- Opening wide: one small island with **seventeen fitted seed-tiles**, one already open; Go geometry is etched into two of the remaining pieces. Leave generous blank sky around the single island. Do not try to make seventeen separate surfaces readable in a fast macro.
- Present-day cast: sixteen people total, one initially active and fifteen emerging. Cameo births happen in two spatially organized groups. The reserved seventeenth tile is the unlabelled future companion.
- No backing slab exists under the tiles. Tiles hold together through edge joints; widening gaps make the remaining assembly precarious.
- Folded bridges, costume strips and graph panels are **inert rescue material**, with no footprint. They transfer weight temporarily and stay attached to an identifiable owner.
- The final tile is the last **rigid** support, not the only object in the universe. Slings, bodies and cloth remain. Phrase the ending accordingly.

The sixteen product figures should not silently include AlphaGo if an additional historical person is introduced. Prefer AlphaGo as the opening stone's named historical motif and Transformer as the assembly's architecture. If a human AlphaGo cameo is required, increment the island population visibly, or put that figure outside the tile birth system from the start. Do not create an uncounted seventeenth contemporary person in a crowd render.

For production, a simple count ledger is enough: opening occupancy; first pair; six core figures; first cameo group; full contemporary group; final companion. Every broad reveal must match that ledger. The viewer need not count seventeen; the production must.

## 3. The chronology currently calls two different things “final”

`cinematography.md` describes the 17.61–19.67 ankle hold as a shoe above the final fragile foothold. `production_design.md` retains the final oval until 30.68, and `episode.md` wakes it at 34.21. Calling both final breaks the countdown.

At 17.61 use **a precarious narrow bridge over a gap**, not the final tile. One visible sleeping region still lies ahead. The camera stop lets us see the depleted island and the fact that contact is dangerous. At 30.68 make the reserved oval unambiguously the only remaining rigid area, fully exposed in one wide. The 34.21 touch then pays off a newly clear choice.

The current 22.70 reveal that “each piece of floor is another potential friend” comes too late if the rule has already been demonstrated twice. Change that reveal to **the cast's larger collective predicament**: the island is nearly gone, and the many earlier births are still present. New information is scale, not the rule repeated as a twist.

## 4. Catching one another cannot itself produce flight

The ending promises “keep the whole world airborne,” but everybody catching everybody supplies no lift. Fantasy can change physics, but this particular fantasy has taught contact transformation, not antigravity.

Use one economical physical cause: **an updraft and a canopy**. In the opening, a detached paper curl rises past the stones. At Claude's first action, the long paper sling visibly pulls upward before it catches anybody. That gives the audience two small pieces of evidence. In flight, the cast spreads the joined sling into a canopy, opening a clean silhouette above their heads. Final birth removes the last rigid support; the characters commit their weight to the already established lifting material.

No new wind-machine character, mystical power aura or tutorial is needed. This is theatrical physics, but it has a rule and an early sign. If the selected art direction favors a spring rather than a canopy, make Claude's strip store visible tension and launch the cast; then end during the earned airborne arc rather than claim indefinite levitation.

The final new person must remain a person with a readable human face and body. Do not solve lift by turning AGI into a giant faceless wing or god, which would break the user's adorable personification request and reintroduce a banned future-deity image.

## 5. Sixteen names fit; sixteen introductions do not

43.670 seconds can sustain one rule, a pair relationship, two ensemble expansions, a brief stop and a payoff. It cannot support sixteen separate “look at this brand, understand its power, read its meme” entrances while preserving that story.

Keep **ChatGPT and DeepSeek** as the only continuous emotional pair. Four others receive one functional action each. Ten cameos appear in two groups of five, introduced across longer continuous action phrases rather than ten little commercials. Names can remain beside their people as the action travels, at full readable size. Do not put five equal labels over five moving faces in one 0.5-second shot.

At least 4–5 seconds of the complete film must allow the ensemble count and spatial relationship to read, distributed over two or three wide views. This is compatible with internal movement at high speed. Reduce isolated label inserts before shortening those geography shots.

The future beat has four conceptual names—AGI, ASI, SGI and singularity—competing for roughly nine seconds with a final birth, aerial choreography and series mark. **Show one speculative doorway/object, not four future characters.** Let two brief graphic states carry the labels while the unlabelled person remains the story. SGI must read `SGI?`; the question mark is cheaper and more accurate than a new invented definition.

## 8. The cinematic rule is narrower than the requested technique display

“A camera passage through a cutout is the single route into a new medium” risks making every transition the same. Keep a **shared contact point and conserved silhouette** as the rule, while allowing different implementations: hard match cut, raster replacement, split-plane wipe, physical occlusion, projection boundary, and a single depth inversion. The transition changes medium; the transformation consumes one tile. These are related but not identical events.

The camera need not physically pass through a cutout every time. Otherwise the requested variety becomes a portal compilation. Let one birth occur in a stationary locked view; its material transition can be the whole spectacle. Let another happen while the camera runs ahead. The 17.61 hold then stops a learned action rhythm rather than merely stopping endless portal travel.

Retain one unmistakable face/hairstyle/accessory/colour signature across media. Global palette conservation is insufficient when sixteen small figures share ivory and blue. Exact names should be authored overlays in safe screen space; don't make generated garments carry spelling tests during a fast transformation.

## 9. Honest status of the benchmark

The written promise can exceed a particle-demo benchmark through physical performances, compositing depth, material changes, exact graphic timing and a coherent reversal. It has **not yet demonstrated** that it is visually more elaborate or more mesmerizing than the referenced video. Six independent look frames cannot establish transition continuity, particle quality or the experience of 43.670 seconds.

At the current art-direction boundary, the appropriate success claim is: selected visual mechanism, attractive still evidence, production methods assigned, and testable risks identified. A later 2–3-second cross-medium contact proof is the decisive test before a large motion batch. This is not a request to begin that batch now.
