# Camera and temporal design

The camera is a participant trying to keep a foothold. Its normal behaviour is lateral tracking at hand/waist height, with an obvious horizon and screen-left to screen-right progress. It does not orbit every character. Opening: floor-height 24mm, contact macro 85mm, return to 35mm readable ensemble. Wide shots establish gaps before anyone crosses them.

A camera passage through a cutout is one route into a new medium; match the contact point within 5% of frame width across the cut. Macro detail never substitutes for seeing source and destination of a leap. Full bodies have floor/air below shoes and room above heads.

The 17.61–19.67 phrase is a locked 50mm photographic two-shot: shoe on the narrow inert rim, with the reserved tile below; hand not yet touching. Everything busy is removed. Music continues. This is a visual hard stop, not an unauthorized edit of the song.

At 22.70 a 24mm rise reveals what earlier close views concealed: each piece of floor is another potential friend. The final top-down reveal is earned by the cast's vertical motion; it happens once.

Master design: 16:9, 1920×1080 review, eventual 3840×2160 delivery if native plates support it, 60 fps timeline. Photographic/video sources retain their true cadence; 24 fps footage is not advertised as native 60 fps. Cel performance may update at 12 fps, paper at 8–12, pixel at 10, procedural camera/particles at 60. Cadence is an authored layer choice, not dropped frames. No optical flow across cuts or transformations.

Fast actions use directional streaks confined to the moving material, not whole-frame blur over text. Keep names held at least 1.2–1.9 seconds and only one or two introduced per action phrase. Longer ensemble reveals can name several without expecting instant recall. No full-screen strobe as a substitute for speed.

Use four transition families, each earned by action: contact-contour match, physical folded-set wipe, pose/cadence match and one perspective inversion. Do not repeat the cutout passage at every conversion. Flight always retains canopy/harness evidence in wides; close coverage must inherit the same taut line direction.
