## Decision

Proceed with the present story. Its strongest property is the consequence after the decision: sharing a useful kitchen means continuing to encounter another person's inconvenient work. The cloth and vegetables are more persuasive than a statement about affection. Keep the divorce, both separate homes, and the unresolved practical friction. Do not add a crisis to enlarge the stakes or a romantic gesture to make the ending reassuring.

The stakes remain deliberately modest. A viewer seeking an urgent speculative thriller may find a kitchen arrangement slight. That is an accepted artistic choice, not a missing catastrophe to insert during production. The scale comes from ordinary people doing an ordinary move inside impossible civic architecture.

## Attacks and smallest remedies

### 1. “They are still a couple commuting between two homes.”

The image alone could support this reading. The locked line in shot 07 explicitly says divorce; shot 14 says they agreed on separate kitchens. Eda then occupies her own room alone in shot 32. These three facts establish an actual separation without a confession scene.

**Keep:** intelligible delivery of the divorce line, her completed move, and task-directed performances. Neither actor seeks a shared look at the end. The two final doorway views must be visibly different homes, not decorative windows in one apartment.

**Reject:** wistful departure, hands touching over the plan, a smile exchanged after the salt, or a final music swell that supplies reconciliation absent from the script.

### 2. “The houseletter is decorative; I never understood what changed.”

The opening must provide a causal chain: packed old room through the left doorway → address tab actually moved → the same doorway now reaches the ring bedroom → Eda reaches back toward the same marked bowl. A generated picture of hands on paper is insufficient. The bowl's crooked white stripe gives the viewer a simple object to track.

**Implemented in selected states:** k02 is an authored before/after paper operation; k03 preserves the kitchen-side jamb and safe floor; k04 shows the empty marked blue bowl and the one cherry in Sen's hand. One room and one object remain available from different addresses. No explanatory narration is needed if these states are legible.

**Acceptance check:** view shots 01–06 consecutively, without relying on the production notes. If the change reads as a new kitchen or a cut to another couple, repair the matched geography. Adding lore to the locked dialogue would be the wrong repair.

### 3. “The small kitchens are absurd toys, so the choice is rigged.”

The modules are designed as real counter-height furniture, 45 cm wide, 45 cm deep and 90 cm tall; selected framing preserves floor-standing scale. Their compact tops are inconvenient; neither product is defective. A pan may safely exceed the diameter of its heating disc. The story cannot depend on it balancing dangerously or being impossible to use.

**Implemented:** selected module plates show floor-standing furniture. k11 shows the pan safely centred. The tray exceeds the small sink, producing a thin patch of water on wood, not a flood, destroyed bedding or an electrical gag. The generous original counter remains visible behind them. They could choose the private modules and adapt; they elect to retain the larger shared surface instead.

**Accepted limit:** they might eventually buy other furniture. The film concerns the arrangement they choose today. Do not invent poverty, a legal ban or a magical limit to close every hypothetical alternative.

### 4. “The staging invents an appliance feature to manufacture a joke.”

The approved module reference has plain cupboard fronts and no shallow drawer. The original drawer beat therefore failed reference continuity.

**Fixed:** shot 19/k14 is now the narrow remaining strip beside the pan, with Eda's pointing hand. Her off-screen “Put it down” is answered by Sen's “Where?” The sound is a light contact, with all drawer sounds removed by the audio owner. No dialogue or timing changed.

### 5. “The choice has no visible cost.”

The ending must show that neither has obtained a private full-size kitchen. Eda occupies the preparation surface; Sen's vegetables are displaced onto a chair. His need to cook and her need to cut both remain reasonable. The small salt exchange requires cooperation, but does not eliminate the inconvenience.

**Implemented:** k22 establishes the occupied counter; k25 locates the one marked bowl on the chair; k29 proves its retrieval; k30 shows the salt physically weighting a pattern corner; k26 completes the exchange with a spare cup replacing the salt as weight. The selected ending preserves the required object chain and keeps cloth clear of the right hob.

**Reject:** a perfectly tidied final kitchen, Eda moving all her work away for him, Sen abandoning cooking, or a decorative two-shot in which the objects no longer obstruct anything.

### 6. “Why does Eda go through the sorting hall if her new door already works?”

The functioning doorway is established early. The later hall passage must therefore read as the public completion of a move, not a newly revealed transport requirement. A person may have access to a new home before finishing their moving business.

**Staging instruction:** show Eda with her secured houseletter and cloth, proceeding through an inhabited public place. Do not add a launch, gate opening, delivery countdown or claim that the packet must still travel before her door can connect. One hall wide is enough; a transport montage would expose an unnecessary mechanics question. Her next independent-room shot is the outcome.

### 7. “The film has one tempo despite shorter cuts.”

The module sequence must alter all four dials: occupied hands, actual placements, shorter shots and denser recorded contacts. The exact ten-second decision hold at frame 2616 stops the performed music and action rhythm once, leaving kitchen air. The quieter ending retains practical activity; it is not another version of the same solemn pause.

**Keep:** the2–3 second module burst, the uninterrupted hold, restrained dialogue and the final longer shots. Natural object impacts are not additional designed silence events. End-card sound fades softly rather than creating a second hard stop.

**Superseded spatial claim:** the earlier review stated that “reused dialogue plates prove shot/reverse-shot geography and accommodate final voices.” The geography claim is withdrawn after the two observed failing cuts. Retained production guidance: avoid universal pushes or simulated camera drift to disguise problems, and judge any later approved motion in full takes rather than inferring it from attractive stills.
