Eda and Sen are dividing their home. In their world a room can have doors at several postal addresses, but it remains one room: nothing inside is duplicated. They can leave with two tiny private kitchens, or keep the full kitchen shared and give up exclusive control of its use. They choose the room, complete their move to separate homes, and immediately get in one another's way.

This is a practical separation without a villain, hidden wound or romantic reunion. Both people have work, preferences and futures. Their familiarity can be irritating and useful in the same gesture. The ending demonstrates the cost they chose: Eda's pattern cutting occupies the worktop while Sen tries to cook. The room does not turn their two households back into one.

## Form and source material

The kitchen composition becomes a dependable geographic reference while its two addresses diverge. Exact object continuity—the cherry bowl, pan, table and lamp—makes a shared room legible.
