# CONTINUITY — screenplay (v1 story reel)

INTERREGNUM, Episode One. Main cut 4:02, alt tail 0:10. Timecodes are `m:ss` from the start of the reel; `[NN]` is
the shot number. Every cut lands on a whole second (the clock ticks at 60 BPM). Speakers and scratch voices:
FATHER = `Daniel`, IDA = `Samantha`, NANA = `Moira`, PA = `Karen` (details in `audio/pilot/cues.md`).

---

**COLD OPEN**

BLACK.

`0:00` [01] **THE NATIONAL IDENT** (4:3). An old black-and-white film, scratched from 41 years of nights: in a black
studio a spotlight finds a ceremonial lamp, a steel ring on a stand. A taper, the wick catches, the flame stands:
*the Lamp*. Three bell notes, warm and official: A… F♯… D.

> ON SCREEN: THE EVENING ADDRESS · 21:00

`0:05` [02] **THE FATHER** (4:3, broadcast). An old man with a white beard and kind, deep-set eyes, framed
dead-center against deep blue, a round studio lamp glowing behind his head like a full moon. After the worn film,
he is perfectly smooth. He looks straight at us. Nothing about him moves, like a portrait that has learned to talk.
A small emblem in the corner; LIVE.

> **FATHER**
> Good evening, my children.
> *(beat)*
> The harvest is in. The sea is calm.

A hymn-like pad underneath, sweet and certain.

`0:12` [03] **CLOSER.**

> **FATHER**
> I am well.
> *(a long beat; then, oddly tender)*
> Eat something warm before you sleep.

`0:19` [04] **FREEZE.** The picture tears for three frames and stops dead. The sound tape-stops into silence. The
picture takes the curve of glass, and we pull back out of it: he is on a hooded monitor at a desk, and a woman's hand
holds a pen of light a centimetre from the glass. A beam draws a mesh over the old man's face, point by point. His
left ear smears into a ghost of itself; a red flag marks it.

> ON SCREEN: L EAR · DRIFT +3.2 PX
> ON SCREEN (burned into the picture): REPLAY 211 · 20:51:07:14

The black bars have closed in from top and bottom. A click of the pen's button. Somewhere, a clock starts to tick.

---

**1. NIGHT DESK 4**

`0:22` [05] **INT. THE HALL, MINISTRY OF CONTINUITY — NIGHT.** A concrete cathedral in the dark. Rows of desks
under dust sheets recede toward a wall of monitors a hundred screens wide, and on it the frozen face, twenty metres
tall, still wearing its wireframe. Far below, one desk is lit. One small woman sits there with an amber scarf. That
is IDA (27). Rain on a roof we cannot see. The clock.

`0:30` [06] **AT THE GLASS.** Her light pen finds the ghost ear through the glass and drags it home.

> **IDA** *(V.O., under her breath)*
> Hold still.

The red flag redraws cold: LOCKED. Down a column of lamps beside the tube, tiny corrections clack from red to white:
MOLE · L CHEEK, BLINK INTERVAL, SKIN TONE, BREATH CYCLE, VOICE WARMTH. Above them, flaps count down: TO AIR 08:38…
08:37…

`0:37` [07] **IDA**, lit cold by the tube, the light pen raised. She is precise, detached and very tired. Behind
her on the desk sits a small dented amber thermos, unopened.

`0:41` [08] **THE ARCHIVE READER.** From above: frames of the Evening Address race across a light table on warm
black-and-white film, a month to a frame, the year tabs flicking past: YEAR 1… YEAR 20… YEAR 41. The drums roll to
CAPTURED 14,763, and the strip brakes and stops.

> ON THE FILM, in grease pencil: LAST LIVE CAPTURE · 10 MAR

A sub-bass drop, then silence. Beside it, a scope's trace lies flat, and a red lamp lights: NO SIGNAL. Its light
finds a paper tag on the knob, in a doctor's hand:

> ON THE TAG: 11 MAR · 04:12

A thin steady tone. The strip moves on over a splice, and the new frames are cold: the copy. A second counter rolls
upward: GENERATED 001… 050… 150… 212. It stops on a clear, empty frame of warm light, boxed in grease pencil: 212 ·
TONIGHT.

`0:50` [09] BLACK.

> TITLE: **CONTINUITY**

---

**2. NO AGREEMENT**

`0:54` [10] **A BRASS PNEUMATIC CRADLE.** A rumble travels through the pipes. A black capsule with a red seal shoots
out and slams into the cradle. Dust. A hiss of air.

`0:57` [11] **IDA'S HANDS** hold open a narrow carbon copy on onionskin, a Committee form ruled in red, still curled
from the capsule. We read it as she does:

> ON PAPER:
> NIGHT 212
> NO AGREEMENT.
> NO TEXT TONIGHT.
> LET HIM SPEAK.
> THE OPERATOR ANSWERS FOR CONTENT.

A red rubber stamp, a little crooked: the Lamp in a ring of dots.

`1:03` [12] **THE SCRIPT TERMINAL.** A red lamp: FREE. She lets him speak. The machine writes the Father on her
terminal, word by word, and his voice reads it:

> **FATHER** *(generated)*
> Good evening, my children. The harvest is in. The sea is calm. I am well. Sleep safely; I am watching over you.
> I am well. The sea is calm. I am well. I am well. I am well—

It loops. The text floods the tube, and beside it a teleprinter hammers I AM WELL. I AM WELL. onto paper that spills
over the desk. The voice multiplies into a choir of itself, rising. A needle stands pinned at 0.99.

A red **HALT**. Silence.

`1:11` [13] **IDA**, lit from below by the frozen text.

> **IDA** *(quiet)*
> No, you're not.

`1:15` [14] **THE AIR CLOCK**, high on the wall: **20:56 · TO AIR 04:00**

> **PA** *(filtered, echoing)*
> Four minutes.

---

**3. THE WARM WINDOW**

`1:17` [15] **EXT. THE CITY — NIGHT, RAIN.** Across a black canal, tower blocks stacked into the distance, hundreds
of windows glowing the same cold blue: every television is showing the stand-by Lamp and waiting for him. A tram
bell. The stand-by chime, echoing from a thousand sets at once. We drift toward the one window that is warm.

`1:24` [16] **INT. NANA'S ROOM — CONTINUOUS.** NANA (82), small in a worn armchair, faces an old television showing
the Lamp. An amber lamp. Two teacups: hers, and one for the empty chair. Soup on a two-ring stove. Rain on the glass.
A wooden clock ticks. The cream rotary telephone rings.

`1:30` [17] **IDA**, turned away from her monitors, the grey handset of her desk phone at her ear, its cord across
her chest.

> **IDA**
> Nana. Don't wait up tonight.

`1:34` [18] **NANA**, the receiver at her ear, her face half blue from the TV and half gold from the lamp.

> **NANA**
> I always wait up. He's on soon.

`1:39` [19] **IDA** lifts her eyes toward the face she makes.

> **IDA**
> Why do you still watch him?

`1:43` [20] **NANA**, closer. A private smile, eyes on the screen.

> **NANA**
> For the ending.
> *(beat; tender, firm)*
> Eat something warm, love.

`1:49` [21] **ON HER TERMINAL: SIGN-OFFS · DESK 4.** Night after night of the Father's last line scrolls up, in
the machine's cold letters: *Sleep safely. Sleep safely. Sleep safely.* Then, in amber, because she typed them:

> NIGHT 023  Close the window. The wind is sly.
> NIGHT 047  Don't argue with the rain.
> NIGHT 061  Old bread makes good soup.
> NIGHT 088  Leave a light on for whoever comes home late.
> NIGHT 119  Call the ones you miss.
> NIGHT 150  Eat something warm before you sleep.

From Night 150 every line is amber and every line is the same. The scroll slows to the bottom:

> NIGHT 211  Eat something warm before you sleep.
> NIGHT 212  ▍

The cursor blinks. Nana's theme, unfinished.

---

**4. NIGHT 212**

`1:57` [22] **THE AIR CLOCK: 20:59 · TO AIR 01:00.** The minutes flap falls to its red-printed 00.

> **PA**
> One minute.

A pulse starts under everything, one beat per second.

`1:59` [23] **THE HALL — LOW ANGLE.** Ida stands at her desk, a silhouette beneath the colossal face. Above the
wall, the red lamp glows standby. We move toward his eyes.

`2:05` [24] **ON HER TERMINAL: THE SCRIPT FOR NIGHT 212.** A template line, cold and locked: *Good evening, my
children.* The cursor sits on the line below. She types, in amber:

> I

The machine offers, in cold letters: *am well.* A needle rises to 0.99. She doesn't take it. The cursor blinks.
She types:

> I died

The suggestion stutters (*am we—*) and vanishes. The needle drops to zero: NO PREDICTION. She finishes:

> I died in the spring.

A red lamp lights on the terminal: VIEWING. Somewhere in the hall a red telephone starts to ring. It will not stop.

`2:13` [25] **IDA'S EYES**, lowered to the keys, lit from below. She doesn't look up. She keeps typing.

`2:16` [26] **ON SCREEN.** More lines are written:

> I'm sorry I stayed so long.
> Tomorrow, you'll have to talk to each other.

She types *Eat*, and this time the machine completes it, in amber, the only new thing it has ever learned:
*something warm before you sleep.* The needle climbs to 0.97. She presses TAB and the line is hers. One more line, a
stage direction:

> [EYES CLOSE]

SCRIPT LOCKED · 6 LINES. TO AIR 00:23.

`2:25` [27] **HER FINGER** above a red-lit key.

> **PA**
> Ten seconds.

The phone rings, the pulse, the rising hum—

`2:29` [28] CUT TO BLACK, then THE IDENT, 4:3, started late, faster and colder: A… F♯… D.

---

**5. THE ADDRESS**

`2:32` [29] **THE FATHER** (broadcast), exactly as every night.

> **FATHER**
> Good evening, my children.

`2:37` [30] **EXT. TRAM STREET — NIGHT, RAIN.** A tram stopped mid-street. A crowd under umbrellas, faces lifted to
the giant screen on a building. Everyone holds still.

> **FATHER** *(from the screen, echoing off the buildings)*
> I died in the spring.

The tram's hum cuts out. Only rain.

`2:43` [31] **THE FATHER**, close.

> **FATHER**
> I'm sorry I stayed so long.

`2:48` [32] **THE HALL.** Ida at her desk, looking up at him, lit by him. The red phone rings on.

> **FATHER** *(huge, from the wall)*
> Tomorrow, you'll have to talk to each other.

`2:54` [33] **NANA**, a cup of tea held under her chin, the TV's light on her face.

> **FATHER** *(from the small TV speaker)*
> Eat something warm before you sleep.

The corners of her mouth lift. Three notes of her theme, and then—

`3:00` [34] **THE FATHER** looks at us a moment longer, then slowly closes his eyes. He does not open them. Dead
air. The hall clock stops ticking.

`3:06` [35] **THE RED SIGNAL LAMP** clicks off. The hall's hum winds down to nothing.

---

**6. COME HOME**

`3:08` [36] **THE RED PHONE** rattles in its cradle, loud in the silence. Ida's hand lies beside it and does not
move.

`3:12` [37] **HER DESK PHONE**, the grey one, rings: a small amber lamp flashes beside a card where she has
pencilled a name:

> ON THE CARD: NANA

`3:15` [38] **IDA** answers the grey phone. In front of her, out of focus, the red one keeps ringing.

> **IDA**
> Nana—

`3:18` [39] **NANA.** The TV is dark now; only the lamp lights her.

> **NANA**
> I know, love.

`3:22` [40] **IDA.**

> **IDA**
> How long?

`3:25` [41] **NANA**, amused and tender.

> **NANA**
> Since he started telling me to eat.

`3:30` [42] **IDA**, the handset against her shoulder, holds the amber thermos she hasn't touched all night. Nana's
theme plays whole for the first time.

> **NANA** *(V.O., on the phone)*
> Come home. The soup's still warm.

She unscrews the cap. Steam rises into the cold light, and we hear her laugh.

`3:37` [43] **THE HALL — THE SAME WIDE AS BEFORE.** The desk is empty and the chair pushed back. On the Wall there
is no face, only the standby Lamp, dim, and the ghost of his face burned into the tubes. The red phone rings far
below, for no one. We pull away.

`3:43` [44] **EXT. THE CITY — FIRST LIGHT.** The rain has stopped. The same towers and the same thousand blue
windows, and Nana's warm one. Then, starting from hers, the windows change one by one from screen-blue to
lamplight. People switch the television off and a lamp on. The wave spreads across the city. The sky turns a
color we have not seen all night: rose. Dripping gutters, a first tram, birds, and under it all, rising, the sound
of a whole city talking.

`3:53` [45] BLACK. Words arrive one at a time:

> *The old is dying and the new cannot be born.*
> — Antonio Gramsci

They fade. The chime returns in a new key, two notes, and the third never comes.

> TITLE: **INTERREGNUM**
> EPISODE ONE · CONTINUITY

`4:02` END.
