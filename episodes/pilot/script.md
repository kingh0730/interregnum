# CONTINUITY — screenplay (v1 story reel)

INTERREGNUM, Episode One. Main cut 4:02, alt tail 0:10. Timecodes are `m:ss` from the start of the reel; `[NN]` is
the shot number. Every cut lands on a whole second (the clock ticks at 60 BPM). Speakers and scratch voices:
FATHER = `Daniel`, IDA = `Samantha`, NANA = `Moira`, PA = `Karen` (details in `audio/pilot/cues.md`).

---

**COLD OPEN**

BLACK.

`0:00` [01] **THE NATIONAL IDENT** (full frame). Deep broadcast blue. A cyan circle draws itself, a flame rises
inside it and a base line slides under it: *the Lamp*. Three bell notes, warm and official: A… F♯… D.

> ON SCREEN: THE EVENING ADDRESS · 21:00

`0:05` [02] **THE FATHER** (full frame, broadcast). An old man with a white beard and kind, deep-set eyes, framed
dead-center against deep blue, a soft halo of light behind his head. He looks straight at us. Nothing about him
moves, like a portrait that has learned to talk. A small emblem in the corner; LIVE.

> **FATHER**
> Good evening, my children.
> *(beat)*
> The harvest is in. The sea is calm.

A hymn-like pad underneath, sweet and certain.

`0:12` [03] **CLOSER.**

> **FATHER**
> I am well.
> *(a long beat; then, oddly tender)*
> Eat something warm before you sleep.

`0:19` [04] **FREEZE.** The picture tears for three frames and stops dead. The sound tape-stops into silence. A cyan
wireframe snaps onto the old man's face, point by point. His left ear smears into a ghost of itself; a red box
flags it.

> ON SCREEN: L EAR · DRIFT +3.2 PX
> ON SCREEN (timecode): CALIBRATION · REPLAY NIGHT 211 · 20:51:07:14

Black bars slide in from top and bottom. A mouse click. Somewhere, a clock starts to tick.

---

**1. NIGHT DESK 4**

`0:22` [05] **INT. THE HALL, MINISTRY OF CONTINUITY — NIGHT.** A concrete cathedral in the dark. Rows of desks
under dust sheets recede toward a wall of monitors a hundred screens wide, and on it the frozen face, twenty metres
tall, still wearing its wireframe. Far below, one desk is lit. One small woman sits there with an amber scarf. That
is IDA (27). Rain on a roof we cannot see. The clock.

`0:30` [06] **ON IDA'S SCREEN: THE CONTINUITY SUITE.** Her crosshair finds the ghost ear, grabs it and drags it home.

> **IDA** *(V.O., under her breath)*
> Hold still.

The red box turns cyan: LOCKED. A list of tiny corrections ticks through: MOLE POSITION, BLINK INTERVAL, SKIN
TONE, BREATH CYCLE, VOICE WARMTH +2%. A countdown in the corner: TO AIR 08:38… 08:37…

`0:37` [07] **IDA**, lit cyan, stylus raised. She is precise, detached and very tired. By her keyboard sits a small
dented amber thermos, unopened.

`0:41` [08] **ON SCREEN: ARCHIVE · SUBJECT F.** Thumbnails of the Evening Address race past, warm and sepia, a
month to a frame: YEAR 1… YEAR 20… YEAR 41. The counter rolls to CAPTURED 14,763, and the strip brakes and stops.

> ON SCREEN: LAST LIVE CAPTURE · 10 MAR

A sub-bass drop, then silence. In the corner a vitals panel draws a flat line.

> ON SCREEN: NO SIGNAL SINCE 11 MAR · 04:12

A thin steady tone. The strip moves on, and the new thumbnails are cyan: GEN, GEN, GEN. A second counter clicks
upward: GENERATED 001… 050… 150… 212. An empty dashed frame: 212 · TONIGHT.

`0:50` [09] BLACK.

> TITLE: **CONTINUITY**

---

**2. NO AGREEMENT**

`0:54` [10] **A BRASS PNEUMATIC CRADLE.** A rumble travels through the pipes. A black capsule with a red seal shoots
out and slams into the cradle. Dust. A hiss of air.

`0:57` [11] **IDA'S HANDS** unroll a narrow slip of paper. We read it as she does, line by line:

> ON PAPER:
> NIGHT 212
> NO AGREEMENT.
> NO TEXT TONIGHT.
> LET HIM SPEAK.
> THE OPERATOR ANSWERS FOR CONTENT.

A red wax seal: the Lamp.

`1:03` [12] **ON SCREEN: FREE GENERATION.** She lets him speak. The machine writes the Father, word by word, and
his voice reads it:

> **FATHER** *(generated)*
> Good evening, my children. The harvest is in. The sea is calm. I am well. Sleep safely; I am watching over you.
> I am well. The sea is calm. I am well. I am well. I am well—

It loops. The text fills the panel, then the screen: I AM WELL. I AM WELL. The voice multiplies into a choir of
itself, rising. Confidence P = 0.99.

A red **STOP**. Silence.

`1:11` [13] **IDA**, lit from below by the frozen text.

> **IDA** *(quiet)*
> No, you're not.

`1:15` [14] **CLOCK: 20:56 · TO AIR 04:00**

> **PA** *(filtered, echoing)*
> Four minutes.

---

**3. THE WARM WINDOW**

`1:17` [15] **EXT. THE CITY — NIGHT, RAIN.** Across a black canal, tower blocks stacked into the distance, hundreds
of windows glowing the same cold blue: every television is showing the stand-by Lamp and waiting for him. A tram
bell. The stand-by chime, echoing from a thousand sets at once. We drift toward the one window that is warm.

`1:24` [16] **INT. NANA'S ROOM — CONTINUOUS.** NANA (82), small in a worn armchair, faces an old television showing
the Lamp. An amber lamp. Two teacups: hers, and one for the empty chair. Soup on a two-ring stove. Rain on the glass.
A wooden clock ticks. The cream rotary telephone rings.

`1:30` [17] **IDA**, turned away from her monitors, a small phone at her ear.

> **IDA**
> Nana. Don't wait up tonight.

`1:34` [18] **NANA**, the receiver at her ear, her face half blue from the TV and half gold from the lamp.

> **NANA**
> I always wait up. He's on soon.

`1:39` [19] **IDA** lifts her eyes toward the face she makes.

> **IDA**
> Why do you still watch him?

`1:43` [20] **NANA**, closer. A private smile, eyes on the screen.

> **NANA**
> For the ending.
> *(beat; tender, firm)*
> Eat something warm, love.

`1:49` [21] **ON SCREEN: `signoffs.txt`.** A plain file. Night after night of the Father's last line scrolls up:
*Sleep safely. Sleep safely. Sleep safely.* Then, in amber:

> NIGHT 023  Close the window. The wind is sly.
> NIGHT 047  Don't argue with the rain.
> NIGHT 061  Old bread makes good soup.
> NIGHT 088  Leave a light on for whoever comes home late.
> NIGHT 119  Call the ones you miss.
> NIGHT 150  Eat something warm before you sleep.

From Night 150 every line is amber and every line is the same. The scroll slows to the bottom:

> NIGHT 211  Eat something warm before you sleep.
> NIGHT 212  ▍

The cursor blinks. Nana's theme, unfinished.

---

**4. NIGHT 212**

`1:57` [22] **CLOCK: 20:59 · TO AIR 01:00** (in red now).

> **PA**
> One minute.

A pulse starts under everything, one beat per second.

`1:59` [23] **THE HALL — LOW ANGLE.** Ida stands at her desk, a silhouette beneath the colossal face. Above the
wall, the red lamp glows standby. We move toward his eyes.

`2:05` [24] **ON SCREEN: SCRIPT · NIGHT 212.** A template line, locked: *Good evening, my children.* The cursor sits
on the line below. She types:

> I

The machine offers, in grey: *am well.* PREDICTION 0.99. She doesn't take it. The cursor blinks. She types:

> I died

The grey suggestion stutters (*am we—*) and vanishes: NO PREDICTION. She finishes:

> I died in the spring.

Top right, a red dot: COMMITTEE — VIEWING. Somewhere in the hall a red telephone starts to ring. It will not stop.

`2:13` [25] **IDA'S EYES**, wet, lines of text reflected in them. She keeps typing.

`2:16` [26] **ON SCREEN.** More lines are written:

> I'm sorry I stayed so long.
> Tomorrow, you'll have to talk to each other.

She types *Eat*, and this time the machine completes it, in amber, the only new thing it has ever learned:
*something warm before you sleep.* PREDICTION 0.97. She presses TAB and the line is hers. One more line, a stage
direction:

> [EYES CLOSE]

SCRIPT LOCKED · 6 LINES. TO AIR 00:23.

`2:25` [27] **HER FINGER** above a red-lit key.

> **PA**
> Ten seconds.

The phone rings, the pulse, the rising hum—

`2:29` [28] CUT TO BLACK, then THE IDENT, full frame, faster and colder: A… F♯… D.

---

**5. THE ADDRESS**

`2:32` [29] **THE FATHER** (broadcast), exactly as every night.

> **FATHER**
> Good evening, my children.

`2:37` [30] **EXT. TRAM STREET — NIGHT, RAIN.** A tram stopped mid-street. A crowd under umbrellas, faces lifted to
the giant screen on a building. Everyone holds still.

> **FATHER** *(from the screen, echoing off the buildings)*
> I died in the spring.

The tram's hum cuts out. Only rain.

`2:43` [31] **THE FATHER**, close.

> **FATHER**
> I'm sorry I stayed so long.

`2:48` [32] **THE HALL.** Ida at her desk, looking up at him, lit by him. The red phone rings on.

> **FATHER** *(huge, from the wall)*
> Tomorrow, you'll have to talk to each other.

`2:54` [33] **NANA**, a cup of tea held under her chin, the TV's light on her face.

> **FATHER** *(from the small TV speaker)*
> Eat something warm before you sleep.

The corners of her mouth lift. Three notes of her theme, and then—

`3:00` [34] **THE FATHER** looks at us a moment longer, then slowly closes his eyes. He does not open them. Dead
air. The hall clock stops ticking.

`3:06` [35] **THE RED SIGNAL LAMP** clicks off. The hall's hum winds down to nothing.

---

**6. COME HOME**

`3:08` [36] **THE RED PHONE** rattles in its cradle, loud in the silence. Ida's hand lies beside it and does not
move.

`3:12` [37] **HER OWN PHONE** lights up on the desk, warm amber:

> ON SCREEN: NANA · calling…

`3:15` [38] **IDA** answers the small phone. Behind her, out of focus, the red one keeps ringing.

> **IDA**
> Nana—

`3:18` [39] **NANA.** The TV is dark now; only the lamp lights her.

> **NANA**
> I know, love.

`3:22` [40] **IDA.**

> **IDA**
> How long?

`3:25` [41] **NANA**, amused and tender.

> **NANA**
> Since he started telling me to eat.

`3:30` [42] **IDA** closes her eyes. A tear, and then a laugh breaks through the crying. Nana's theme plays whole
for the first time.

> **NANA** *(V.O., on the phone)*
> Come home. The soup's still warm.

`3:37` [43] **THE HALL — THE SAME WIDE AS BEFORE.** The desk is empty and the chair pushed back. On the monitor wall
there is no face, only the Lamp, dim. The red phone rings far below, for no one. We pull away.

`3:43` [44] **EXT. THE CITY — FIRST LIGHT.** The rain has stopped. The same towers and the same thousand blue
windows, and Nana's warm one. Then, starting from hers, the windows change one by one from screen-blue to
lamplight. People switch the television off and a lamp on. The wave spreads across the city. The sky turns a
color we have not seen all night: rose. Dripping gutters, a first tram, birds, and under it all, rising, the sound
of a whole city talking.

`3:53` [45] BLACK. Words arrive one at a time:

> *The old is dying and the new cannot be born.*
> — Antonio Gramsci

They fade. The chime returns in a new key, two notes, and the third never comes.

> TITLE: **INTERREGNUM**
> EPISODE ONE · CONTINUITY

`4:02` END.
