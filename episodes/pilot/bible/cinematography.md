# Cinematography: the pilot's camera language

The series-wide camera and acting rules are in `../../../bible/visual.md`. This file is how the pilot applies them,
plus the pilot's own choices. Where it repeats a series rule, the series file wins.

How the pilot is shot: where the camera stands, which lens, how it frames and lights, when it moves, and how
actors in a video model are directed. It applies to all three art directions (`art_direction.md`) and uses the
hardware of `production_design.md`. The rules replace the "lens language" and "camera moves" notes in
`episodes/pilot/episode.md`.

The v1 reel failed on three counts. Nearly every shot was a centred, eye-level medium shot. Every still drifted on a
2–10 % push, which is the tell of a slideshow. And faces carried the emotion, which video models then exaggerate. The
fixes are below.

---

## 1. Whose eye is the camera?
Every setup belongs to someone. Decide whose eye it is before choosing a lens.

| Where | Whose eye | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| The Hall, wides | **The institution's:** high, on the axis, symmetrical, far away, locked or on rails | Ida is watched. The gallery could be looking from here |
| Desk 4, close | **A colleague's:** at her side, slightly above her eye line, never below | The job, the hands, the tiredness |
| The Father | **The nation's:** the lens *is* the viewer | He is the only person who looks into the lens |
| Nana's flat | **A guest's:** at seated eye height, close, patient, never above her | She is at home, and we are visiting |
| The city | **A neighbour's across the canal:** a long lens from the opposite bank at the height of Nana's floor | One window among thousands, and we know which |
| The street | **The crowd's:** at head height among the umbrellas | Everyone is watching the same thing |
| Hardware inserts | **The operator's eye line,** or straight overhead for paper and the archive | Objects are evidence |

## 2. Frame shapes
- **World:** 2.39:1, letterboxed in the 1920 × 1080 master. Keyframes keep all action in the band y 138–942.
- **Broadcast:** 4:3, pillarboxed (1440 × 1080, centred). This is the only frame the Father ever has
  (`production_design.md` §7).
- **The change of shape is always an event.** Shot 04 turns the 4:3 picture into a tube in a 2.39 room. Shots 28–34
  alternate between them during the Address. After 34 the film never returns to 4:3 (except in the alt tail, 46).

## 4. Composition

1. **Negative space is the scale.** In wides the figure takes ≤ 3 % of the frame. In close-ups the face takes ≤ a
   quarter of the frame width, and the rest is darkness or the light's source.
2. **Nobody alive is centred.** Only the Father sits dead-centre, because symmetry belongs to the state and the dead
   man. When Ida stands up under the face (23), she breaks the Hall's symmetry, and that is an act.
3. **Ida is short-sided until she is free.** In the Hall she looks toward the near edge of the frame with the empty
   space behind her head: trapped by the machine. Nana always gets lead room, open space in the direction she looks.
   **In shot 42, for the first time, Ida gets lead room.** The frame opens when she stops carrying it alone.
4. **Silhouette first.** Every figure reads as a dark shape against light or a light shape against dark: Ida's bob and
   scarf, Nana's bun and round glasses, the Father's head in its disc of light. Check it with the threshold test (§9).
5. **Three planes.** A foreground occluder (a desk cover, a pipe, an umbrella, a lampshade, the red phone), the
   subject, and a light source behind. The occluder says someone is watching, and it gives Seedance real parallax.
6. **Frames within frames.** The state boxes people with its objects: the Wall's niches, the Proof's hood, the
   television niche in Nana's wall unit, window mullions, canal railings, the gallery glass. Use one in most shots.
   The last shot breaks out of the grid: in 44 the grid itself changes colour.
7. **Lines carry the story.** The Hall's axis joins the desk, the face, the lamp and the clock. The brass ledger lines
   run toward the Wall. **The phone cord** is the line to Nana: it crosses Ida's frame in every call shot, from the
   handset at her ear to the desk.
8. **Detail at one point.** Each frame has one sharp, detailed focal point, and everything else is simplified or dark.

## 5. Light design per location

| Location | Key | Other sources | Never |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Hall** | the Wall, a huge cold area source 40 m from Desk 4: long shadows down the nave, a lit floor plane | the ON AIR lamp and the gallery's pilot lamp (red points); floor grilles (a faint cold up-light carrying the dust); Desk 4's tubes | fill light; warm brass; haze below the desks (haze lives in the nave's upper half) |
| **Desk 4, close** | the tube she's facing: cold, low, from the side she looks toward | the Wall as a cold back-rim; the amber call lamp and the thermos as small warm accents | light on the side of her face away from the tubes |
| **Nana's flat** | two keys: the lamp (tungsten, low, left of her chair) and the television (cold, low, right) | the window's weak blue as a far back-light; the lampshade's hard arc on the wall and its hot ring on the ceiling | an overhead light; any third colour |
| **The city** | the windows themselves | low cloud lit from below; mercury street lamps; the Public Receiver's hot spot and spill; the Transmitter's red blink | sodium light; moonlight; rain drawn against black (rain shows only where it crosses light) |
| **Dawn (44)** | the sky, rose from below the horizon on the undersides of the clouds | the windows turning amber; the beacon still red | direct sun |
| **The broadcast studio** | a soft frontal key and a round backlight disc behind his head | a cold rim on hair and shoulders | shadows; texture. It is the only soft, even light in the film (the Copy Rule) |

**The light story (keep it continuous across the cut):**
- Ida is lit cold from the first frame.
- Her own typed words put the first warm light on her fingers (21).
- The amber ghost puts it on the screen (26).
- Nana's call puts it on her cheek (37–42). As the Wall drops to standby, the cyan on her hair fades until only the
  warm light is left.
- Nana's face holds both lights until the Address ends. From 39 on, only the lamp lights her.

## 6. When the camera moves

These are the pilot's choices: its home register is stillness. The series rule they sit under (familiarity,
variety and surprise, used sparingly) is `../../../bible/visual.md` §1 and §5.

In v1 nearly every still got a 2–10 % push, so every shot drifted and no single move meant anything. In v2 the
motion lives inside the frame, so the camera can stop.

1. **Locked-off is the default.** Everyone in this film is watching something, and the frame holds still with them.
   No micro-pushes on faces.
2. **The camera moves only when the story moves:** a reveal, a decision, a release. Each move has one direction, one
   speed and one reason.
3. **Institutions move like machines; people's world breathes.** Moves in the Hall run on rails at constant speed,
   straight and on the axis. Moves in the city ease in and out, like a breath.
4. **Never** pan across a flat image, zoom (nobody in this film looks harder), or go handheld.
5. **One mechanism per move.** A move is either comp on a plate (≤ 10 % scale, with parallax layers) or one plain
   instruction to Seedance ("the camera dollies slowly forward along the aisle at a constant speed"), never both.

**The pilot's moves (everything else is locked):**

| Shot | Move | Why | Feel |
|---|---|---|---|
| 04 | pull out of the broadcast into the room | Reveal 1: he is a picture on a tube | a mechanical ease-out |
| 05 | dolly down the nave toward Desk 4 | the institution finds her | rails, constant speed |
| 22 | short push on the red 00 | the pulse begins | accelerating |
| 23 | push past her into his eyes | her decision | accelerating |
| 27 | push onto the fingertip | the commitment | accelerating, then a hard cut |
| 32 | pull back from Ida | her words fill the building; the world widens | rails, constant speed |
| 43 | the exact reverse of 05 | the empty desk; the institution lets her go | rails, constant speed |
| 44 | pull back from Nana's window across the city | release | the only move that breathes: a long ease |

## 7. Shot-size grammar across a scene

- **The ladder.** A scene opens wide (where we are, and who is watching), then steps in one shot size per exchange,
  and saves its tightest size for the line that turns the scene. It never skips two sizes on a cut, except for the
  film's punctuation: a jump from a wide to an insert.
- **The mirror.** In the phone scenes Ida and Nana are mirror images: the same lens, the same shot size, the same eye
  height in frame, opposite screen directions (Ida faces screen-left, Nana screen-right). Their eye lines meet across
  the cut.
- **The reaction is an object.** After a line, cut to what the character holds or looks at, not to a face: the
  thermos, the cord, the red phone, the cup, the clock. The audience supplies the emotion (Kuleshov).
- **Inserts** are always real objects, at the operator's eye line or straight overhead. They last as long as it takes
  to read, plus one tick.

**In the pilot:**
- **Scene 1, NIGHT DESK 4:** 05 extreme wide (the institution), 06 insert (the work: a pen on glass), 07 MCU (the
  worker), 08 overhead insert (the evidence). Wide, hands, face, evidence.
- **Scene 3, THE WARM WINDOW:** 15 the city (300 mm), 16 the room (32 mm), 17 Ida MCU, 18 Nana MCU, 19 Ida CU, 20
  Nana CU. The tightest size is saved for "For the ending." The ladder converges, then 21's insert of the evidence
  pays it off.
- **Scene 6, COME HOME:** 36 insert (the red phone and her still hand: the refusal), 37 insert (the House Line lamp:
  the answer), then 38 Ida CU, 39 Nana CU, 40 Ida tighter CU (a new keyframe, not a digital punch-in), 41 Nana
  tighter CU. **42 holds 40's size but opens the frame:** she gets lead room at last. Then 43 cuts to the widest
  frame in the film, the empty desk. The truth arrives as the ladder tightens, and the release is a wide.

## 8. Acting for video models: behaviour, not emotion

Seedance reads an emotion word as an instruction to perform it and turns it up. "Stunned, eyes glistening" comes
back as a soap-opera close-up. The emotion has to come from what surrounds the face: the cut before and after, the
sound, the light and the objects.

### The rules
Rules 1–8 are series rules now: `../../../bible/visual.md` §6. The pilot adds one:

9. **The Father is already right.** "Minimal head movement, one slow blink, an almost unnaturally smooth stillness"
   is the Copy Rule in motion. Keep it.

### The Seedance style line
A clip inherits its look from the start frame, so a motion prompt carries one short style line, not the Codex block:
- **STILL LIFE:** "Hand-painted gouache animation; keep the first frame's exact colours, brushwork and designs."
- **RELIEF:** "Colour woodcut print animation; keep the first frame's exact carved shapes, flat inks and designs."
- **TABLEAU:** "Stop-motion puppet animation on a miniature set; keep the first frame's puppets, materials and light."
  Hold the result on twos in comp.

### The motion-prompt template
The template is in `../../../bible/visual.md` §6.

### Three rewrites
The pilot's staging changes too: Ida now holds the grey House Line handset, whose cord runs back to the desk
(`production_design.md` §4), not a smartphone. Examples use the RELIEF style line.

**Shot 38 · "Nana—"**
- **Before** (`shots/38/shot.md`): *Flat 2D cel-painted style. Close-up: Ida raises a small phone to her ear,
  stunned, eyes glistening. In the blurred foreground a red desk telephone keeps ringing. She starts to say "Nana—"
  and stops. Static camera. The red telephone bell in a silent hall. No music.*
- **What's wrong:** "stunned" and "eyes glistening" ask for shock and tears. Raising the phone, speaking and stopping
  are three actions in three seconds. And the phone is from the wrong century.
- **After:** *Colour woodcut print animation; keep the first frame's exact carved shapes, flat inks and designs.
  Close-up, 85 mm, slightly above her eye line: Ida in profile facing screen-left, the grey handset of the desk phone
  already at her ear, its coiled cord running down out of frame. In the soft foreground at lower left, the red
  telephone's handset rattles in its cradle. She is still, her eyes lowered to the desk in front of her. She says one
  word, quietly: "Nana—" and stops, her lips staying slightly parted. Her head does not move. Understated performance:
  her face stays composed; only her lips move. Locked-off camera. The red telephone's bell close by in a silent hall.
  No music.*
- **Start frame (k23):** replace "stunned and hollow, eyes glistening, lips parted" with "face composed, eyes lowered
  to the desk, lips parted on the first syllable, the handset at her ear".

**Shot 40 · "How long?"**
- **Before:** *Flat 2D cel-painted style. Close-up: Ida, phone at her ear, barely able to speak, asks: "How long?"
  Her lips tremble; her eyes fill. Static camera. The silence of a vast hall, a distant telephone. No music.*
- **What's wrong:** three emotion instructions ("barely able to speak", "lips tremble", "eyes fill") make the model
  play crying. v1's 1.25× digital punch-in on k23 was also visibly soft.
- **After:** *Colour woodcut print animation; keep the first frame's exact carved shapes, flat inks and designs.
  Close-up, tighter than the last shot: her head and the handset fill the left half of the frame, profile facing
  screen-left. Ida holds the handset to her ear. She is still. She says quietly, lips barely moving: "How long?" Then
  she waits, listening, without moving. At the lower edge of the frame, her free hand rests on the desk and slowly
  closes around the phone cord. Understated performance: her face stays composed; only her lips and that hand move.
  Locked-off camera. The silence of a vast hall, a telephone far away. No music.*
- **Start frame:** a new keyframe at this size (no punch-in), with the same neutral face as 38.

**Shot 42 · COME HOME**
- **Before:** *Flat 2D cel-painted style. Close-up: Ida, a small phone at her ear, closes her eyes; a tear runs down
  her cheek and a laugh breaks through the crying. From the phone, a warm old voice: "Come home. The soup's still
  warm." Static camera, slow push-in. A quiet hall, a faint telephone. No music.*
- **What's wrong:** "a tear" plus "a laugh breaks through the crying" is the model's most exaggerated register, and a
  push-in on a face reads as the camera asking for tears.
- **After:** *Colour woodcut print animation; keep the first frame's exact carved shapes, flat inks and designs.
  Close-up at the same size and height as the last shot, but now she sits with open space in front of her face.
  Ida holds the handset to her ear, listening, still. After two seconds she closes her eyes and keeps them closed. She
  breathes out once, slowly, and her shoulders drop. Her head lowers a little, until her forehead rests against the
  knuckles of the hand holding the handset, and she stays there. Understated performance: her face stays soft and
  composed; nothing else moves. Locked-off camera.* Render it as a silent take (`--no-audio`). Nana's line and Ida's
  breath turning into a small laugh are added in the mix: **the laugh is heard, not seen.**
- **Start frame (k25):** replace "eyes closed, one tear, a laugh breaking through the crying" with "eyes open and
  resting on the desk, face composed, the handset at her ear". The eyes close in the clip.
- **Optional beat (director's call):** a 2 s insert after the line, if the edit can make room on the tick. Her free
  hand unscrews Nana's amber thermos, unopened since shot 07, and steam rises into the cold light. The soup's still
  warm, and her hand says so.

## 9. Checks Claude can run on stills
Claude can't watch video, but every rule above has a still-frame test. Motion needs the showrunner's eyes, and the
handoff must say so plainly.

| Check | Test | Pass |
|---|---|---|
| Silhouette | threshold the keyframe at 25 % luminance | every figure and key object still reads |
| Darkness | share of pixels under 15 % luminance in a night shot | ≥ 50 % |
| Nobody centred | horizontal position of the face's centre (not the Father) | ≥ 12 % of frame width from the centre |
| Palette | hue histogram against the allowed families (for RELIEF, count the inks) | no stray hues; no sodium orange; no warm brass in the Hall |
| Eyes | zoom on the eyes | no specular highlights; no tears in keyframes |
| Lens and height | horizon line and perspective against §3 | matches the table for the setup |
| Motion prompt | scan for the banned words in §8 | none |
